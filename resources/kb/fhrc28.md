## Section 3 — Product Overview

The Etnyre Fixed Hopper ChipSpreader FHRC28 is a self-propelled, computer-controlled hydrostatic chip-spreading machine designed to apply aggregate (chips) to freshly oiled road surfaces with precision application rates. It is engineered to improve the accuracy of chip spreading while improving productivity and simplifying operation through Application Rate capabilities using precise gate opening control and speed feedback in closed loop controls.

**Key Systems:**

- **Drive System:** Hydrostatic ground drive using a Rexroth AA4VG125 main drive pump. Available in 2WD (single front axle drive) or 4WD configuration with front and rear hydraulic motors (107 cc or 160 cc displacement).  
- **Controller:** RC-28 (Rexroth MDC computer), computerized closed-loop control of speed, gate opening, and application rate.  
- **Hopper:** Single fixed-width front hopper with hydraulic cylinder gate actuation. Individual air-operated gates controlled by solenoids. Gate opening range: standard 4.00 inches maximum (5.00 inches on big chipper models).  
- **Spread Roll:** Hydraulically driven at a target speed of 100 RPM, controlled by a flow control valve.  
- **Conveyors:** Two independently controlled rear conveyors (left and right) feed material from the supply truck into the front hopper. Speed adjustable from 10% to 99%.  
- **Augers:** Two independently controlled augers distribute material in the front hopper.  
- **Hydraulic System:** Three hydraulic pumps — (1) main hydrostatic drive pump (Rexroth AA4VG125), (2) auxiliary pressure-compensating pump (Rexroth A10VO74) supplying conveyors/spreadroll/gates/hitch, (3) fixed displacement gear pump supplying power steering and fan.  
- **Air System:** Compressor, reservoir, oiler, water separator, main regulator (set 80 psi), controls individual gate air cylinders.  
- **Hitch:** Hydraulic hitch with automatic latch, height adjustment, and release pushbutton. Optional traction boost cylinder.  
- **Operator Station:** Full control console with joystick (speed/direction), RC-28 display, all gate controls, conveyor controls, engine diagnostics, and optional sliding seat.

| Right Conveyor Selector | ON / AUTO / OFF per need | OFF | OFF |
| Gate/Spread Roll Switch (Thumb Switch) | OFF until at start line | OFF | OFF |
| Gate Master Switch | ON for full-width spreading | ON | OFF |
| Individual Gate Switches (Left/Right) | ON for full-width | As required | OFF |
| Rear Hopper Flow Gate | Open to deliver material to conveyor | — | Closed |

### Conveyor Operating Modes

| Mode | Selector Position | Behavior |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Manual ON | Bottom position | Conveyor runs continuously |
| Auto | Middle position | Conveyor starts when hopper material sensor detects empty, stops when full |
| OFF | Upper position | Conveyor stopped |

**Note:** When operating with the truck, the rear conveyor gates should be set to deliver as much material as possible into the conveyor without spillage, then the conveyor speed adjusted to deliver slightly more aggregate to the front hopper than the amount being spread. When properly adjusted, conveyors should run approximately 80% of the time with the hopper spreading full width at maximum job speed.

**Agitator:** The front hopper agitator can be disengaged for clean, dry aggregate by removing the agitator disconnect bolt. Re-engage the agitator when spreading sand or small aggregate (bridging risk) by reinstalling the bolt. An optional second agitator is available for sand applications.

## Section 6 — System Startup

### Pre-Start Inspection Checklist

Before starting the engine each day, complete the following checks:

1. Inspect tires for proper pressure: **55–60 PSI front, 60–65 PSI rear**. Never exceed maximum inflation pressure shown on the tire sidewall.  
2. Check for fluid leaks (hydraulic oil, engine oil, coolant, fuel) at all visible hoses, fittings, and connections.  
3. Check hydraulic reservoir oil level at the sight eye on the tank.  
4. Check engine coolant level (refer to engine operator's manual).  
5. Check engine oil level (refer to engine operator's manual).  
6. Check fuel level — refuel if below 25% to avoid running the fuel sender alarm (alarm activates at approximately 10% / 7 gallons).  
7. Grease all lubrication fittings in accordance with the Chipspreader Lubrication Chart on the side of the tool box.  
8. Verify all shields, covers, and guards are in place.  
9. Confirm the control joystick is in the **neutral** position.  
10. Confirm the Park/Drive switch is in the **PARK** position.  
11. Verify wheels are chocked if machine is on a grade.  
12. Confirm the emergency stop switch is reset (twist clockwise if it was previously activated).  
13. Check hydraulic suction gate valve is **fully open** (counterclockwise).

>   
> **CAUTION:** Always start and operate the engine in a well-ventilated area. If in an enclosed area, vent the exhaust to the outside. Do not modify or tamper with the exhaust system.  
>   
> **WARNING:** Diesel engine exhaust and some of its constituents are known to the State of California to cause cancer, birth defects, and other reproductive harm. (Proposition 65\)

### Engine Startup Sequence

1. Place the **Park/Drive switch** in the **PARK** position.  
     
2. Verify the **control joystick** is in the **neutral** (center) position.  
     
3. Place the **Throttle Run/Idle switch** in the **IDLE** position.  
     
4. Rotate the **Power/Ignition switch** from OFF to ON — all system power energizes.  
     
5. Wait for the **"WAIT TO START"** indicator to clear on the engine display (if Tier 3/4/5 diesel equipped).  
     
6. Rotate the ignition switch further right (spring-loaded) to the **START** position to engage the starter. Release the key once the engine fires — the switch returns to ON.  
     
   > **CAUTION:** Do not crank for more than 30 seconds at a time. Wait at least 2 minutes before attempting again.  
   >   
   > **NOTE:** The engine will NOT start with the Park/Drive switch in the Drive position. If started without the joystick in neutral, the machine will not move until the joystick is returned to neutral.  
     
7. Allow engine to warm up at **low idle** for a few minutes.  
     
8. Once warmed up, switch **Throttle Run/Idle** to the **RUN** position. The engine will run at high idle. The hydrostatic system is designed to run at high idle for all work.  
     
9. Check the **computer display screen** — the main operator screen will show application rate set point, aggregate size, and speed set point.  
     
10. Confirm **charge pressure** is approximately 400 PSI (visible in service/setup screens if needed).  
      
11. Move the **Park/Drive switch** to **DRIVE** to enable machine movement.

>   
> **CAUTION:** Auxiliary computer power is routed through the Park/Drive switch. When the engine is not running, place the switch in the PARK position to avoid draining the batteries.  
>   
> **WARNING:** Turning the ignition switch to "OFF" results in a violent stop. Shut machine off and wait for all movement to stop before leaving the operator's seat or servicing.

## Section 7 — Operating Procedures

### Normal Chip Spreading Work Cycle

1. **Hook Up to Supply Truck:** Back the chipspreader into the truck; the hitch automatically closes and locks on contact. Place the truck gearshift in neutral as soon as the truck is connected.  
     
2. **Set Aggregate Size:** Press the **Aggregate Size Switch** (4) to select the aggregate being spread (Sand, 1/4", 3/8" Chips, 3/8" Gravel, 5/8" Chips, or 1" Chips). This must be set before adjusting application rate or calibrating.  
     
3. **Set Application Rate:** Use the **Application Rate Switch** (5) to set the desired lbs/yd² set point. Pressing up increases the rate; pressing down decreases it.  
     
4. **Set Travel Speed:** With the joystick in neutral and the machine stopped, use the **Speed Set Toggle Switch** (3) to set the desired FPM (feet per minute). Pressing up increases the speed set point; pressing down decreases it.  
     
5. **Load Memory (Optional):** If this combination of rate/size/speed has been saved to a memory preset, press the appropriate **Memory Selector Button** (1–5) to restore all parameters at once.  
     
6. **Verify Gates OFF:** Confirm the **Gate/Spread Roll Thumb Switch** (10) is in the OFF (left) position.  
     
7. **Start Conveyors:** Set the Left and Right Conveyor Selector switches to AUTO or ON to begin feeding material from the supply truck into the front hopper. Monitor the hopper level — when properly filled, conveyors should cycle on approximately 80% of the time.  
     
8. **Move to Starting Line:** Push the **joystick (9)** smoothly to the full forward position. The machine will accelerate to the set speed. Use the thumb switch detent and adjustable friction to hold desired positions.  
     
   > **WARNING:** Stay off hopper while machine is moving. Machine movements could cause a fall resulting in injury or death.  
     
9. **Activate Gates at Starting Line:** Approximately 10 feet before the start line, depress the RIGHT side of the **Gate/Spread Roll Thumb Switch** (10) to turn gates ON. The spread roll also activates. The computer opens the gate to the position required for the selected speed and application rate.  
     
10. **Spread Material:** Drive at constant speed. The computer continuously adjusts the gate opening to maintain the selected application rate as speed changes.  
      
    - To change speed while spreading: use the **Speed Toggle Switch** — the chipspreader transitions smoothly to the new speed while maintaining the application rate.  
    - To momentarily open gates wider (to clear a jam): press and hold the **Gate Override Switch** (23). Gates return to set position when released.  
    - To turn off individual gate sections: use the **Left/Right Gate Selector Switches** (43, 44\) during operation.

    

11. **Close Gates at End Line:** Depress the LEFT (center) side of the **Gate/Spread Roll Thumb Switch** (10) to close the gates. The spread roll deactivates.  
      
12. **Stop Machine:** Return the joystick to neutral. The parking brake is automatically applied once the speed sensor reads 0 FPM.  
      
13. **Release from Truck (if needed):** Push the **Hitch Release Pushbutton** (17 or 50\) while momentarily pulling the joystick slightly rearward to create slack in the hitch. Push joystick forward again while holding hitch release. Once the truck separates, release the pushbutton.  
      
14. **Park Machine:** Place the **Park/Drive switch** in PARK. Always install the locking control box cover and chock wheels when leaving the machine unattended.

### Speed Behavior Notes

- Gates automatically close when chipspreader speed falls below **30 FPM**, even if the thumb switch remains on. Gates will immediately begin to reopen when speed exceeds 30 FPM again.  
- Full reverse speed requires moving the joystick to the full reverse position. Max reverse speed is up to **1,300 FPM** — to go faster in reverse, increase the speed set point.  
- After backing, reselect the memory button to restore your forward chipping speed.

### Conveyor Hood Adjustment

Conveyor hoods should be adjusted to control material distribution in the front hopper. A 1.5" gap is a good starting point for 3/8" to 1/2" chips. Generally, the larger the stone, the further forward the hood should be positioned.

## Section 9 — Calibration

### Why Calibrate?

Material calibration ensures that the application rate displayed on the screen (lbs/yd²) equals what is actually being applied to the ground. Aggregate density, moisture content, and particle shape vary — a fresh calibration is needed whenever material changes, when the application rate on the ground differs significantly from the set point, or after replacing a gate transducer.

### When to Calibrate

- When starting a new job with a different material  
- When the actual spread rate on the ground differs from the set point by more than 2 lbs/yd²  
- After adjusting or replacing the gate transducer  
- When changing aggregate size on the display

### Material Calibration — Step-by-Step

**Before starting:** Verify the spread roll is turning at 100 RPM. Select the correct aggregate size on the display. Set the application rate set point to the desired value. Set speed set point to approximately 300 FPM. Have the canvas (supplied with machine) and scale ready.

1. Place the canvas on a flat surface with room for the chipspreader to get up to speed.  
2. With the joystick pushed fully forward, maneuver toward the canvas at constant speed.  
3. Activate the gate thumb switch approximately **10 feet before** the material will hit the canvas — this ensures the gate is in the correct position.  
4. Once the canvas is covered, deactivate the gate thumb switch.  
5. Weigh the material and canvas together. Then weigh the canvas empty. Subtract to get the pounds per square yard of material on the canvas.  
6. Repeat a **minimum of 3 times** and average the values before making any changes.

**Entering Calibration into RC-28:**

The FHRC28 uses the **Left Hopper calibration screens** (the program is universal; the fixed hopper uses left hopper screens).

7. Press the CAL switch UP or DOWN to access calibration screens. The screen will show:  
     
   0.0       95.6% lb/yd²    RIGHT CAL  
     
8. Press the **Scroll switch DOWN** to advance past the right hopper screen to the left hopper:  
     
   0.0       95.6% lb/yd²    LEFT CAL  
     
9. Example: If the hopper weighed 23.5 lbs with a 20.0 lbs/yd² set point — measured is 3.5 lbs too heavy. Press and hold the CAL switch DOWN until the lb/yd² display reads **\-3.5**.  
     
   - If material is lighter than desired: press CAL switch UP to enter a positive value.  
   - The LEFT CAL percentage value is a reference density factor.

   

10. Press the **SAVE pushbutton** to store the calibration. This immediately decreases/increases gate output by the entered amount. The screen resets to zero once saved.  
      
11. Press the Scroll switch UP **twice** to return to the main operator screen.

**Fine Tuning While Chipping:**

- If the spread appears lighter than desired: press CAL, scroll to Left CAL screen, toggle CAL UP to raise the value 1–2 lbs, press SAVE. Change takes effect immediately.  
- Calibration is permanent — values are retained when the machine is powered down.

### Conveyor Speed Calibration

1. From any operator screen, press CAL UP or DOWN.  
     
2. Navigate to the Left Conv screen:  
     
   Left Conv 85%  
     
3. CAL UP increases left conveyor speed by 5%. CAL DOWN decreases by 5%. Range: 10%–99%.  
     
4. Scroll DOWN to Right Conv and repeat.  
     
5. Scroll UP twice to return to main screen.

### Auger Speed Calibration

1. Press CAL from main screen, navigate to Left Auger screen.  
2. CAL UP increases left auger speed by 5%. CAL DOWN decreases by 5%. Range: 10%–99%.  
3. Scroll DOWN to Right Auger and repeat.

### Joystick Calibration

Enter Setup screens: hold the CAL switch and turn ignition to ON. Release CAL after computer beeps three times.

1. Navigate to **SETUP: JOYSTICK**. Verify display reads 0.0% with stick in neutral.  
2. Press CAL to begin calibration sequence.  
3. **Neutral:** Press CAL to calibrate neutral position (display shows \~2.4 volts).  
4. **Forward:** Push stick fully forward (display should show \~4.4 volts). Press CAL.  
5. **Reverse:** Pull stick fully to reverse (\~0.7 volts). Press CAL.  
6. Display returns to 0.0% — calibration complete. Press Scroll to advance.  
7. Navigate to **SETUP: SAVE AND EXIT** and press SAVE.

### Gate Transducer Calibration (Left Gate — Fixed Hopper)

Enter setup screens (CAL hold \+ ignition ON, release after 3 beeps). Navigate to LEFT GATE NULL.

1. Verify gate is fully closed — 1/16" clearance between each gate and spreadroll.  
2. At **SETUP: LEFT NULL**: value should read 0.00 inches. If not, press CAL DOWN to set to 0.00.  
3. Scroll DOWN to **SETUP: LEFT SCALE**.  
4. Press and hold the Gate Override pushbutton. Display should show 4.00 inches while gates are fully open.  
5. If the display does not show 4.00, verify gates are physically fully open, then press CAL to set value to 4.00 inches.  
6. Release gate override — display should return to 0.00.  
7. Navigate to **SETUP: SAVE AND EXIT** and press SAVE.

**Physical Transducer Voltage Check (Solid State Type — Red/Blue/Black wires):**

- Red wire to black wire: **5.0 VDC** (supply — fixed, not adjustable)  
- Blue wire to black wire with gate CLOSED: **0.5–1.5 VDC** (ideal: 1.0 VDC)  
- Blue wire to black wire with gate FULLY OPEN: **3.5–4.5 VDC**  
- If not within spec: loosen transducer mounting bolts and rotate transducer until reading is within range. Retighten. Re-run null/scale setup.

**Signal voltage increase across 4-inch gate opening should be approximately 3 VDC.**

### Gate Open/Close Speed (Amp) Adjustment

In computer Setup screens (or Service screens), navigate to LEFT OPEN and LEFT CLOSE:

- LEFT OPEN: 0.900 amps (default). Increasing amps increases gate open speed.  
- LEFT CLOSE: 0.950 amps (default). Increasing amps increases gate close speed.  
- Maximum: **1.2 amps** — do not exceed; above this value gates will hunt/fluctuate.

### Q: The display shows a speed sensor error, or the alarm sounds when the joystick is out of neutral but the machine does not move

**Step 1 — Joystick Neutral Check:** Before anything else, confirm the joystick is in the neutral (center) position. The alarm will sound if the joystick is out of neutral and the machine does not move. Return joystick to neutral; if alarm clears, the sensor was reading correctly and the issue may be resolved.

**Step 2 — Fuses and Circuit Breakers:** At the control console, check all fuses and circuit breakers. Locate the inline 30-amp computer fuse at the main fuse block (right rear area of machine). Replace any blown fuse with one of the same rating — never install a larger fuse.

**Step 3 — Physical Speed Sensor Check (at front motor, Spicer or Kessler axle):** Locate the magnetic pickup sensor on the front axle drive motor output (see Figure 1A/1B in M-218-11R). Check for: (a) loose sensor — sensor must be firmly mounted; (b) poor alignment — sensor must face the gear/pick-up target; (c) improper clearance — recommended gap is **1/16"** between sensor tip and gear; (d) loose wiring connection at sensor; (e) pinched or rubbed wiring along harness route.

**Step 4 — Wiring Check (harness disconnected from sensor):** Using a multimeter, measure at the sensor harness connector:

- Red wire: should read **12 VDC**  
- Black wire: **ground**  
- Clear or white wire (signal): should read **9–10 VDC** at rest  
- Shield: ground If voltages are OK, proceed to Step 5 (harness connected). If voltages are NOT OK, proceed to Step 6 (computer connector check).

**Step 5 — Sensor Output Check (harness connected, machine moving):**

- Red wire: **12 VDC**  
- Black wire: ground  
- Clear/white signal wire: **4–7 VAC while machine is moving** — if no signal, replace the speed sensor.  
  - Kessler axle speed sensor: Part No. **6703670**

**Step 6 — Computer Connector Check (sensor disconnected):**

- At computer connector P1-PIN 21: should read **12 VDC** (speed sensor power)  
  - If OK at computer but no voltage in Step 4 (red wire): check wiring harness continuity between computer and sensor. If no continuity: replace harness.  
  - If no voltage at P1-PIN 21: bad connection or bad computer output.  
- At computer connector P3-PIN 10: should read **9–10 VDC** (signal)  
  - If OK at P3-PIN 10 but no signal in Step 4 (clear wire): check harness continuity. Replace harness if no continuity.  
  - If NO voltage at P3-PIN 10: check voltage at other end of pull-up resistor on P3-PIN 10\. Should be 12 VDC. If 12 VDC is present at one end but not the other: replace the 1k OHM pull-up resistor.  
- Ground: attached to computer base/chassis.

### Q: The display shows a fuel alarm, or the fuel alarm keeps sounding even though I filled up

**Step 1 — Verify Fuel Level:** Check the actual fuel level physically or on the display screen (FUEL % on Screen 2). The alarm activates at approximately **10% / 7 gallons remaining** (usable). If the tank is genuinely low, add fuel and confirm alarm clears.

**Step 2 — Check for Debris on Sender:** Open the fuel tank access and visually inspect for metallic debris lying on top of the sending unit float. Debris can push the float down, falsely triggering the alarm. Remove any debris.

**Step 3 — Wiring Check:** Inspect the wiring from the fuel sending unit to the computer for loose connections, frayed wires, or bare wire touching ground (grounded wiring falsely triggers the low-fuel alarm).

**Step 4 — Sender Isolation Test:** Disconnect the wire from the fuel sending unit.

- If the alarm stops: the sender itself is faulty or out of resistance spec. Refer to the resistance-vs-gallons chart (Figure 2 in M-218-11R). Check sender resistance with a multimeter against the chart values.  
- If the alarm continues: the problem is in the wiring or computer — proceed to Step 5\.

**Step 5 — Wiring Between Sender and Computer:** Check continuity of wiring between the fuel sender and computer. Look for shorts, open circuits, or corroded connectors along the full harness length.

**Step 6 — Computer Connector Check:** Inspect computer connector for loose or corroded pins.

**Step 7 — Computer Terminal Check:** At computer terminal **P2-PIN 19**: verify proper voltage/resistance signal is arriving from the fuel sender. A bad reading here indicates wiring or sender fault. If signal is good but alarm persists, the computer may need service.

### Q: Low oil pressure alarm is on

**Step 1 — Verify with Mechanical Gauge:** Install a mechanical oil pressure gauge on the engine and verify actual engine oil pressure. Expected values:

- **\~30 PSI at idle**  
- **\~65 PSI at full RPM** (engine coolant at operating temperature 160–190°F) If mechanical pressure is low, the engine has an actual oil pressure problem — stop the engine immediately and investigate engine oil level, oil pump, and oil system.

**Step 2 — Sensor Wiring Check:** Inspect wiring at the oil pressure sensor (on engine block) for bad or loose connection. Repair or tighten as needed.

**Step 3 — Sensor Ground Test:** Ground the sensor signal wire directly to the engine block.

- If the alarm stops: the sender is out of resistance specification. Reference the resistance chart (Figure 3 in M-218-11R) and install a new OEM oil pressure sender with correct resistance values.  
  - Alarm triggers at: **5 PSI @ 1400 RPM and below** / **10 PSI @ 1401 RPM and above** (must exist for 2 seconds to activate alarm).  
- If the alarm continues: the problem is in the wiring or computer — proceed to Step 4\.

**Step 4 — Computer Connectors P2 and P3:** Inspect both connectors for loose pins, corrosion, or damage.

**Step 5 — Computer Terminal Check:**

- Oil pressure sender signal: **P2-PIN 18**  
- Oil pressure ground: **P2-PIN 2** Measure signal and ground at these pins. Bad readings indicate wiring fault between sensor and computer.

**Step 6 — Wiring Inspection:** Look for wires pulled out, touching other wires, or disconnected along the entire harness from engine sensor to computer.

**Step 7 — Continuity Check:** Check continuity of wiring between computer and oil pressure sender from P2-PIN 18 all the way to the sender connector.

**Step 8 — Replace Sender:** Install new oil pressure sender — must be OEM replacement with correct resistance values. Aftermarket senders with incorrect resistance will not work properly with the Rexroth computer.

### Q: Water temperature alarm is on / engine is overheating

**Step 1 — Verify with External Gauge:** Use a laser infrared probe or mechanical temperature gauge to verify the actual coolant temperature.

- If NOT exceeding 240°F: the sensor or wiring is falsely triggering the alarm — proceed to Step 2\.  
- If coolant IS exceeding 240°F: the engine is genuinely overheating — jump to Step 7\.

**Step 2 — Sensor Terminal Check:** Inspect the wiring terminal at the coolant temperature sensor (on engine) for a loose or bad connection. Tighten or repair.

**Step 3 — Wiring Inspection:** Check wiring for: (a) frayed wiring; (b) bare wire. Repair as needed.

**Step 4 — Sensor Isolation Test:** Disconnect the sensor.

- If alarm stops: the sender is faulty or out of resistance spec. Refer to resistance chart (Figure 4 in M-218-11R). Expected resistance decreases as temperature increases.  
- If alarm continues: problem is in wiring or computer — proceed to Step 5\.

**Step 5 — Computer Connection P2:** Check for loose or bad connection at computer connector P2.

**Step 6 — Computer Terminal:** Wiring from sensor to computer connects to **P2-PIN 20**.

**Step 7 — Coolant Level:** Carefully remove the radiator cap (use caution — engine HOT) and check coolant level. Add coolant if low. Low coolant is the most common cause of genuine overheating.

**Step 8 — Radiator Debris:** Check and clean any debris (stone chips, dust, mud) from the front of the radiator. Chip spreading generates significant debris accumulation.

**Step 9 — Cooler Plugging:** Inspect the radiator and hydraulic oil cooler for plugging with aggregate dust or particles. Clean if clogged — a plugged cooler severely reduces cooling capacity.

**Step 10 — Cooling System Leaks:** Check for leaks in the cooling system. Leaks allow particles to collect and block passages.

**Step 11 — Investigate Further:** If overheating continues after all above steps, investigate the radiator, water pump, thermostat, and engine internally.

### Q: Hydraulic oil temperature alarm is on

**Step 1 — Oil Level:** Check hydraulic oil level at the sight eye on the hydraulic reservoir. Add oil if low (see Lubrication chart for correct oil type).

**Step 2 — Verify Actual Temperature:** Use a laser probe or mechanical temp gauge at the hydraulic reservoir.

- If oil temperature IS above 180°F: genuine overheating — proceed to Step 3\.  
- If oil temperature is below 180°F: false alarm — skip to Step 9 (sensor check).

**Step 3 — Filter Restriction:** Check the filter restriction indicator gauges on the filter bases (both return filters). A damaged or broken gauge should be replaced. A blocked filter creates heat.

**Step 4 — Hydraulic Cooler Debris (front):** Check for stone chips, dust, or other debris trapped in front of the hydraulic oil cooler. Debris indicates a possible leak in the hydraulic or cooling system. Clean thoroughly.

**Step 5 — Between Coolers:** Check for debris trapped between the hydraulic cooler and the engine radiator — this blocks airflow between both coolers.

**Step 6 — Heat Source Identification:** Try to locate the source of heat generation: manifold blocks, quick couplings, hydraulic fittings, or hoses that are unusually hot to the touch (use caution).

**Step 7 — System Plumbing:** Verify the hydraulic system is plumbed correctly, especially that oil flow runs through the hydraulic cooler on return.

**Step 8 — Hydraulic Pressures:** Check hydraulic pressures to ensure they are not above allowable limits (see Section 2 — Specifications). Pressures above spec generate excessive heat.

**Step 9 — Temperature Sensor Connection:** Check the connection at the temperature sensor on the hydraulic reservoir tank for a loose or bad connection.

**Step 10 — Sensor Isolation:** Disconnect the sensor wire.

- If alarm stops: sensor or wiring is faulty. Reference resistance chart (Figure 4 in M-218-11R — same chart as water temperature sensor).  
- If alarm continues: problem is computer or wiring.

**Step 11 — Computer Connector P2:** Inspect connector P2 for loose pins or corrosion.

**Step 12 — Computer Terminal:** Hydraulic oil temp sensor wiring: **P2-PIN 17**.

**Step 13 — Wiring Short:** Check entire wiring harness from sensor to computer for short to ground.

### Q: Gate sensor failure alarm on display (left or right gate)

**Step 1 — Identify Which Side:** Note which side the digital display indicates — left gate or right gate. On a fixed hopper, only the left gate is used.

**Step 2 — Physical Inspection:** Locate the gate transducer on the indicated side.

- (a) Check if the harness connector is disconnected at the sensor.  
- (b) Inspect for broken or pinched wiring along the transducer harness.  
- (c) Check for bad, corroded, or loose connections at the connector.

**Step 3 — Identify Transducer Type:**

**Solid State (Red/Blue/Black wires):**

- Ensure gate is fully closed.  
- Do NOT unplug the sensor — check voltage at the connector using Etnyre adapter Part \# 7050350 to avoid stripping wires.  
- Red wire to black: should read **5.0 VDC** (supply, not adjustable).  
- Blue wire to black (gate closed): should read **0.5–1.5 VDC** (ideal 1.0 VDC).  
- If not within spec: loosen transducer mounting bolts and rotate transducer body until blue-to-black voltage is 0.5–1.5 VDC. Retighten bolts (see Figure 5 in M-218-11R).

**Resistor Type Potentiometer (Orange/Green/Black wires — older units):**

- Ensure gate is fully closed.  
- Unplug sensor from harness.  
- Check OHMS across orange and green wires at connector: should read **250–270 OHMS**.  
- OHMS increase as gate opens — do NOT exceed 270 OHMS.  
- Make adjustment if not within spec.

**Step 4 — Re-calibrate After Adjustment:** After any transducer adjustment, re-run gate null/scale calibration in computer Setup screens. Material will also need to be re-calibrated.

**Step 5 — Wiring Continuity:** Check wiring continuity between transducer and computer connector.

**Step 6 — Computer Connector Terminals (Rexroth):**

- Fixed hopper / Left gate (+): **P1-PIN 10** (Red)  
- Left gate (−): **P1-PIN 18** (Black)  
- Left gate signal/wiper: **P1-PIN 6** (Clear)  
- Right gate (+): P1-PIN 11 (Red)  
- Right gate (−): P1-PIN 19 (Black)  
- Right gate signal/wiper: P1-PIN 7 (Clear)

### Q: One gate opens or closes faster than the other side

**Step 1 — Adjust Gate Speed in Computer Setup:** Enter setup screens (hold CAL \+ turn ignition ON; release after 3 beeps). Navigate using the scroll switch to the gate speed screens:

- **Right gate open:** Adjust amps up/down. Higher amps \= faster gate. DO NOT exceed **1.2 amps**.  
- **Right gate close:** Adjust if needed (1.2 amps max).  
- **Left gate open:** Adjust if needed. On a fixed hopper, this is the primary gate speed control.  
- **Left gate close:** Adjust if needed (1.2 amps max).

Note: On fixed hopper, left gate open/close controls the gate speed. Variable width units: if amps are not the same between left and right, gate fluctuation can occur.

**Step 2 — Hydraulic Pressure Check:** Check and verify hydraulic pressure at the gate relief valve. If pressure is not at 1,500 PSI, adjust per gate relief valve procedure (see Section 8 of this KB).

**Step 3 — Wiring Connections at Gate Valve:** Inspect wiring connections at the gate solenoid valve connector:

- Terminal 1 (Red wire): power to valve  
- Terminal 2 (Black wire): ground

**Step 4 — HON Connector Check:** Locate the Harting (HON) connector on the left outside conveyor rail (and under right conveyor above right front tire for variable units). Check for loose, corroded, or damaged connections.

**Step 5 — Computer Output to Gate Valve:** Measure amp output to the gate valve and compare to the threshold set in computer setup. Output should match. Check computer connector terminals:

- P4-PIN 5: Right gate open solenoid (+)  
- P4-PIN 6: Right gate close solenoid (+)  
- P4-PIN 3: Left gate open solenoid (+)  
- P4-PIN 4: Left gate close solenoid (+)  
- Ground post (−) for all above.

### Q: Very loud noise when the gate master power is turned on or off (air gates out of adjustment)

**Step 1 — Identify Source:** Determine if this is an air gate problem or a hydraulic gate buss arm problem.

- Loud bang/click at all gates simultaneously \= likely hydraulic buss arm issue → go to Step 3\.  
- Noise at individual gates \= air gate adjustment issue → go to Step 2\.

**Step 2 — Adjust Air Gates:**

a. Back off ALL hydraulic gate buss arm adjustment screws between all hydraulic gate buss arms and air gates. b. Adjust air gate adjustment screws to achieve a **uniform 1/16" gap** across the entire spread roll. Ensure air pressure is applied, forcing the gate closed during this adjustment. c. Check all buss arm assembly bolts — they must be tight. Replace any bent or broken bolts. d. Re-calibrate gate null/scale in computer setup (see Section 8). e. With machine running and hydraulic pressure applied to the hydraulic gate buss arm: turn air gate master power ON, turn all individual air gate switches ON, and allow air gates to open to the hydraulic gate buss arm. f. Adjust hydraulic gate buss arm adjustment screws, forcing air gates to close until the air gate adjustment screws touch and STOP movement. Do NOT force screws past contact — this will change the transducer setting. g. Re-verify gate null is 0.00 and scale is 4.00.

**Step 3 — Adjust Hydraulic Gate Buss Arm:** a. Re-calibrate gate null/scale in computer setup first. b. If transducer adjustment is also needed, refer to the gate sensor failure procedure above.

### Q: The spread roll speed is fluctuating / speed is not steady

**Step 1 — Auxiliary Standby Pressure:** Check the auxiliary pump standby (load sense) pressure. If too low, the pump will not come on stroke fast enough, causing insufficient pressure and flow to the spread roll. Verify standby pressure is set to **400 PSI**. Adjustment procedure is in the operation manual (see Appendix D of M-213-16 and Section 8 of this KB).

**Step 2 — Spread Roll Speed Adjustment:** Check and adjust the speed of the spread roll (target **100 RPM**) at the flow control cartridge. On standard fixed hopper units, this cartridge is located in the gate/spreadroll manifold (between the conveyors).

- Turn cartridge adjuster CLOCKWISE to decrease spread roll speed.  
- Turn COUNTERCLOCKWISE to increase speed.

**Step 3 — Flow Control Cartridge Inspection:** Remove and inspect the spread roll flow control cartridge for contamination or debris. Clean thoroughly and reinstall.

**Step 4 — Pressure vs. Speed Correlation:** Monitor auxiliary standby pressure with a gauge while watching spread roll speed. If spread roll speed follows pressure fluctuations (speed drops when pressure drops), this confirms a load sense issue. Proceed to Step 5\.

**Step 5 — Load Sense Check Valves:** Remove the two load sense check valves located on TOP of the conveyor manifold (standard units) and inspect for contamination, debris, worn springs, or pitted seat assemblies. Replace as needed.

### Q: Hydraulic standby / load sense pressure is fluctuating (needle on gauge is not steady)

This occurs when the volume of oil in the load sense system is too great, due to a missing or contaminated orifice or load sense check valve.

**Step 1 — Orifice Inspection (at auxiliary pump):** Locate the load sense line fitting attached to the auxiliary hydraulic pump compensator valve (see Figure 9 in M-218-11R).

- (a) Verify the orifice is installed inside the fitting — it should be a set screw with a **.040 inch hole** drilled through it.  
- (b) Check the orifice for contamination or clogging.  
- (c) Verify the orifice size is .040 inch.

**Step 2 — Check Compensator Operation:** Verify the compensator is responding to hydraulic demand by checking standby pressure with engine running.

**Step 3 — Isolate Load Sense Check Valves:** a. Shut engine off. Remove the load sense lines from the compensators and cap them off. b. Start engine and check standby pressure — if pressure is now steady at 400 PSI, proceed to step (b). If pressure is still erratic, the pump or compensator is the issue. c. Reconnect load sense lines ONE AT A TIME to identify which manifold block contains the contaminated check valve. d. Remove and inspect the bad load sense check valve. Clean or replace.

### Q: Gates will not operate (hydraulic buss bar does not move)

**Step 1 — Arming Switches:** Verify that the gate arming switches (gate selector switches on operator panel behind joystick) are turned ON. Without the arming switches on, the thumb switch cannot open the gates.

**Step 2 — Fuses/Circuit Breakers:** Check ALL fuses and circuit breakers. Verify there is power on BOTH sides of each fuse holder or circuit breaker — a blown fuse will have power on only one side.

**Step 3 — Gate Override Test:** Press the gate override button and check if the gate operates.

- Gate OPERATES with override: there is an electrical issue → proceed to Steps 5, 6\.  
- Gate does NOT operate with override: there is a hydraulic issue → proceed to Step 4\.

**Step 4 — Hydraulic Gate Valve:** Manually override the gate valve spool assembly to test hydraulic operation.

- Gate OPERATES with mechanical override: electrical issue → proceed to Step 5\.  
- Gate does NOT operate with mechanical override: check hydraulic pressures (see Appendix D of M-213-16).

**Step 5 — Gate Transducer / Potentiometer:** Check gate pot/transducer adjustment per Problem 6 and 7 procedures above.

**Step 6 — Gate Null/Scale Calibration:** Verify gate null/scale calibration per operation manual setup procedure.

**Step 7 — Joystick Center Roller Micro Switch:** Check the joystick center roller micro switch — verify it is activating when the joystick is pushed out of neutral.

**Step 8 — Gate Switch in Joystick:** Check power across the gate switch (thumb switch) in the joystick handle — verify it is activating (shows power when pressed).

**Step 9 — Relay Check:** Inspect relays \#2, \#3, and \#4:

- Relay \#2: activates when joystick is pushed out of neutral (micro switch connects), then allows relays \#3 and \#4 to activate with arming switches ON and gate switch ON.  
- Relay \#3: activates the LH gate.  
- Relay \#4: activates the RH gate. Verify gate arming switches are ON during relay testing.

**Note on Gate Hold Feature (variable hopper):** The RH gate uses a gate hold feature set in computer setup screens, designed for straight-line start when spreading rock. If the FPM reading is malfunctioning, the hold feature may prevent the RH gate from opening. Disable the gate hold (set to 0 in setup screens) to allow RH gates to operate in manual mode.

### Q: The starter cranks but the engine will not start

**Step 1 — Display Check:** Does the display show "ENGINE CAN" (no CAN bus communication)?

- YES → proceed to Step 2\.

**Step 2 — Emergency Stop Button:** Check the emergency stop button (Ref 40\) — verify it was not accidentally bumped or pushed in. Twist it clockwise to pop out and release.

**Step 3 — Engine Controller:** Cummins QSB and Tier 3 engines have an Engine Controller that requires power to start. Check if this is present on the machine.

**Step 4 — ECM Fuses:** Locate the Cummins ECM controller fuses near the power and ground distribution hub at the right rear corner of the Cummins engine. There may be up to 6 fuses depending on engine size.

- Verify fuse ratings against the machine schematic.  
- Replace any blown fuse with one of the SAME rating — never use a larger fuse.  
- **Special note for yellow rubber-insulated glass fuse holders:** Pull holder apart and confirm the ring is over the END of the fuse (not just touching the metal part). Ensure a good solder joint at the wiring end. This type of holder can appear intact while actually losing contact under load.

**Step 5 — E-Stop Switch Connections:** Check the E-Stop switch connections for a possible wiring issue related to the fuel solenoid circuit.

**Step 6 — Fuel Solenoid:** Check for an issue with the fuel solenoid on the Cummins engine. If the solenoid is not energizing, fuel cannot reach the injectors.

### Q: Machine stops while I am moving and then moves again when I push the joystick to neutral and back forward

This indicates the neutral joystick micro switch is opening while the joystick is out of neutral — the computer thinks the joystick is in neutral and applies the parking brake.

**Step 1 — Micro Switch Adjustment:** Locate the neutral micro switch on the joystick assembly (see Figure 14 and Figure 15 in M-218-11R). Adjust the neutral micro switch UP so the switch does NOT open when the joystick is out of neutral. The switch must remain closed within the first 5% of joystick movement in both forward and reverse.

### Q: Machine will not go into reverse

**Step 1 — Reverse Micro Switch Check:** The reverse micro switch on the joystick must actuate within the first **5% of joystick movement** in reverse. Locate the reverse micro switch (Figure 14/15 in M-218-11R).

- Adjust the reverse micro switch DOWN to make it activate sooner.  
- Or adjust the neutral switch to ensure both the neutral and reverse circuits complete within 5% of joystick movement in reverse.  
- The reverse switch (Red/White wire after update) must receive **12 VDC** within 5% of joystick movement in reverse.  
- The neutral switch (Purple wire) must lose **12 VDC** within 5% of joystick movement in forward and reverse.

### Q: Machine will not move forward or in reverse after a Rexroth computer update

**Step 1 — Calibrate Joystick:** After any computer update, the joystick must be re-calibrated.

1. With ignition OFF, hold CAL switch DOWN and turn ignition to ON. Wait for 3 beeps, release CAL.  
2. Navigate setup screens to **SETUP: JOYSTICK**.  
3. Press CAL DOWN once — screen changes to neutral calibration.  
4. Press CAL DOWN again — screen changes to forward calibration.  
5. Push joystick FULL FORWARD until display shows 4.0–4.9 volts. Press CAL DOWN.  
6. Pull joystick FULL REVERSE — display should show \-4.0 to \-4.9 volts. Press CAL DOWN.  
7. Return joystick to neutral. Screen returns to joystick calibration screen.  
8. Navigate to SAVE AND EXIT screen. Press SAVE.

**Step 2 — Micro Switch Adjustment:** If machine still does not move after joystick calibration:

1. Turn ignition ON. Watch the main display screen after computer boots.  
2. Push joystick forward (out of neutral) — the speed set point display should change to **0**. If it does not change to 0, the neutral micro switch is not activating within 5% — adjust the neutral switch closer to the activating cam.  
3. Push joystick in reverse — speed set point should again change to 0\. If not, adjust both neutral and reverse switches as described in the reverse switch procedure above.

### Q: No gradability — machine cannot climb the hill

**Step 1 — Grade Calculation:** Verify the actual % grade of the incline against the machine's rated gradability at maximum combined weight (80,000 lbs):

- 2WD: **6–8%**  
- 4WD with 107cc motors: **8–12%**  
- 4WD with 160cc motors: **12–18%** Motor size (cc) is stamped on a tag attached to the top of the drive motor. If the grade exceeds the machine's capability, the machine CANNOT climb it — do not attempt to force it.

**Step 2 — 2WD Machines — Servo Connector Test:** Disconnect the electrical connector attached to the front drive hydraulic motor servo (secure wire away from moving parts). Test the unit on the grade with 80,000 lbs total weight. If performance improves, the servo was receiving incorrect signal — reconnect and further diagnose servo control circuit.

**Step 3 — 4WD Machines — Servo Connector Test:** Disconnect electrical connectors from BOTH front and rear drive hydraulic motor servos. Test on grade at 80,000 lbs. If performance improves, reconnect servos one at a time to identify which motor servo is receiving bad signal (the internal swash plate of the hydraulic motor is being incorrectly positioned by the servo signal).

**Step 4 — POR Pressure Check:** Check and verify the hydrostatic priority override (POR) hydraulic pressure. Target: **6,500 PSI**. Low POR means the system destokes before reaching full torque.

**Step 5 — Forward High Pressure Check:** Check main hydrostatic high pressure (forward). Target: **7,000 PSI**. Low forward relief pressure means reduced drive force.

**Step 6 — Reverse High Pressure Check:** Check main hydrostatic high pressure (reverse). Also target **7,000 PSI**.

### Q: Emergency — Machine is broken down in the road and cannot move

Use the Emergency Driveline Disengage Procedure (M-218-11R). This is for emergency use only — to move the machine extremely slowly off the road or onto a transport trailer.

**Step 1:** Install two 04MB x 04MJ fittings (Etnyre Part \# 9410200\) — one in each test port of the high-pressure distribution block bolted to the side of the drive pump (remove the 04MB plugs and install new fittings on both sides of the pump).

**Step 2:** Install one 04 x 65-inch hydraulic hose with 04FJX fittings (Etnyre Part \# 3181497\) across between the two fittings. This creates a loop on the drive system, bypassing the pump and relief system.

**Step 3:** Disable or release the park brake unit (mounted in front of the drive motor on the front axle).

**Step 4:** If the engine does NOT run (or the pump cannot supply brake release pressure): use a porta-power modified with a 04 FJX connection to supply approximately **280–400 PSI** to the parking brake unit to release it.

**Step 5:** If the engine RUNS: place controls in Drive mode, push joystick in direction of movement, and steer as needed. Move the machine VERY SLOWLY (crawl speed only) off the road or onto the trailer.

**WARNING:** This procedure is for emergency situations ONLY. Not intended for travel or long-distance movement. Damage can occur if not done correctly.

### Q: MDC fault code shows "Invalid Reset"

**Cause:** Some part of the MDC resets without a full CPU reset — typically an internal fault.

**Step 1:** Check if P3-pins 11, 12, 13, and/or 14 are shorted to ground. If 3 or 4 of these pins are grounded simultaneously, an invalid reset occurs.

**Step 2:** Check wiring to the back light of the display for a short or ground fault.

---

### Q: MDC fault code shows "Invalid Speed"

**Cause:** Speed feedback is reading greater than 2,500 FPM — beyond the computer's valid range.

**Fix:** Check for a bad speed pickup (sensor) or electrical noise on the sensor signal line. Check sensor clearance (should be 1/16") and wiring shielding.

---

### Q: MDC fault code shows "Internal Voltage"

**Cause:** One of the regulated voltages (especially the \+5 VDC supply) is out of range.

**Fix:** The \+5V supply powers old-style displays, the joystick, and gate pot power. Check if \+5V is shorted to ground. If a circuit is drawing more than 150 milliamps (old display), this fault will also trigger.

---

### Q: MDC fault code shows "Invalid Program"

**Cause:** Program was not loaded properly into the MDC, or parts of the program have become corrupt.

**Fix:** Reload the program software to the MDC.

### Q: Auxiliary hydraulic pressure will not relieve

This is typically discovered when checking auxiliary pump pressures. If the main pressure does not relieve, the auxiliary pump cannot destroke — this causes heat buildup, engine torque loss, and hard starting.

**Step 1:** Shut off machine.

**Step 2:** Close and shut off the main hydraulic suction valve.

**Step 3:** Locate and cap off the case drain at the auxiliary pump.

**Step 4:** Remove the compensator from the hydraulic pump(s).

**Step 5:** Remove the load sense compensator cartridge.

**Step 6:** Locate the 2mm allen head orifice inside the housing (see Figure 12 in M-218-11R). Remove the orifice.

**Step 7:** Clean the orifice using a strand of primary wire or a torch cleaning kit (if strand is small enough to fit). Do NOT enlarge the orifice.

**Step 8:** Re-install orifice, cartridge, and compensator in reverse order.

**Step 9:** Uncap and reinstall the case drain hose.

**Step 10:** Open the suction valve.

**Step 11:** Install an air regulator to the hydraulic tank through the vent filter connection. Apply **3–5 lbs of pressure** to the hydraulic system to push any trapped air out.

**Step 12:** Start machine and operate all hydraulic functions to ensure full oil circulation. Check and adjust pressures per the operation manual. Verify pressure relieves after main pressure is achieved.

**Note:** If pressure cannot be adjusted on the pump after this procedure, the pump may have sustained internal damage and will need to be replaced. Isolate each pump individually to identify which pump is affected.

## Section 11 — Troubleshooting — Output Quality Problems

### Q: There is an overlap or heavy streak of material down the center of the road when the hopper is fully extended

**Cause:** The material is heavy in the middle — the gate cut-off plate needs to be adjusted inward.

**Fix:**

1. Locate the gate cut-off plate in the hopper (see Figure 10 in M-218-11R).  
2. Slide the cut-off plate **further IN** (toward center) on both sides evenly.  
3. Adjust both sides equal amounts to keep material distribution symmetric.

---

### Q: There is a void or streak down the center of the road

**Cause:** Not enough material reaching the center. The gate cut-off plate is too far in, or the hopper position is too narrow.

**Fix:**

1. Adjust the gate cut-off plate by sliding it **further OUT** of the hopper.  
2. If the cut-off plate cannot be adjusted further out, adjust the hopper cylinder rod end to extend the hopper width (both sides evenly).  
3. See Figure 11 in M-218-11R for cylinder adjustment reference.

---

### Q: The application rate on the ground does not match what the display shows

**Cause:** Material calibration is needed, or the wrong aggregate size is selected.

**Fix:**

1. First verify the correct aggregate size is selected on the display (Section 8).  
2. Perform the full material calibration procedure (Section 8\) with the canvas and scale.  
3. Average at least 3 canvas samples before adjusting calibration.  
4. If calibration cannot bring rates to match within 2 lbs/yd², check for gate wear plate adjustment needs or gate transducer issues.

---

### Q: Gates are delivering material at low speeds but not maintaining proper rate at high speeds

**Cause:** The gate opening is at its maximum 4-inch travel for the aggregate size before reaching the required application rate — the computer cannot open the gate further.

**Fix:**

1. Reduce the speed set point — lower speed requires smaller gate opening, giving the computer more range to control rate.  
2. Check if the maximum gate opening is set correctly in Setup screens (SETUP: GATE OPENING — should be 4.00" for standard units, 5.00" for big chipper).  
3. Check gate wear plates — if worn, the effective gate opening is reduced.

---

### Q: Material is bridging in the hopper — flow stops intermittently

**Cause:** Aggregate is arching across the hopper and stopping flow.

**Fix:**

1. Ensure the hopper agitator is engaged (agitator disconnect bolt installed).  
2. For sand or small aggregate: install the optional second agitator.  
3. Verify the agitator is rotating — check the agitator drive connection.  
4. Increase conveyor speed to push more material through and break the bridge.  
5. Use the Gate Override button to momentarily open gates fully — this can dislodge bridged material.

## Section 12 — Troubleshooting — Application-Specific Problems

### Q: Machine loses traction on a wet or freshly oiled surface

**Fix:**

1. Activate the **Traction Control Switch** (31) — this transfers torque to the front axle. The joystick must be in neutral to engage it. Speed is limited to 200 FPM while engaged. Move the machine into better traction, then disengage.  
2. If available, activate the **Traction Boost Switch** (45) — this pushes up on the hitch to add weight to the rear drive wheels (only effective when connected to a truck). Note: this reduces truck braking efficiency.  
3. Adjust tire pressure within the specified range (55–60 PSI front, 60–65 PSI rear).

---

### Q: Machine cannot hold position on a grade / rolls back when stopping

**Cause:** Parking brake may not have engaged because the speed sensor did not read zero (machine still moving slightly when stopped).

**Fix:**

1. Use the **Brake Pedal** (47) to hold the machine on the grade until the parking brake sets.  
2. Place the **Park/Drive switch** in PARK position — this applies the parking brake positively.

>   
> **WARNING:** Parking Brake meets SAE J1472. Parking brake may NOT hold on grades steeper than 15%. On such grades, chock the wheels.

---

### Q: Spreading on shoulders — material not distributing evenly to the shoulder edge

**Fix:**

1. Use the individual gate selector switches (43, 44\) to turn off gates that do not cover the shoulder — concentrate material flow to the working side.  
2. Adjust the left and right conveyor speed independently to control where material accumulates in the hopper.  
3. Adjust the conveyor hood position to direct material distribution toward the shoulder side.

---

### Q: Truck is having trouble keeping up / truck is pushing the chipspreader on a downgrade

**Fix:**

1. Under most conditions, the chipspreader should tow the truck.  
2. On steep downgrades where the truck needs to assist in braking, the truck should always set its own brakes after stopping — regardless of grade orientation.  
3. Establish a clear communication system (radio, hand signals, horns) between chipspreader and truck. Each truck driver must know who gives signals, where to look, and what each signal means.  
4. To disconnect from the truck: push Hitch Release button while momentarily pulling joystick rearward to create slack, then drive forward while holding the release button.

---

### Q: Stopping distances are too long / machine cannot stop quickly enough

**Fix:**

1. Slow down — use lower speed set points when towing heavy trucks, on steep grades, or when material is loaded.  
2. Use the brake pedal to supplement hydrostatic braking.  
3. Avoid loading the front hopper or rear hopper when roading the machine — weight increases stopping distance significantly.  
4. The larger the truck and steeper the grade, the longer the stopping distance.

## Section 15 — Winterizing / Storage

No specific winterizing procedure is published in the M-213-16 manual. The following is based on general hydraulic/engine machine best practices consistent with the manual's maintenance guidance:

1. **Drain/flush the engine cooling system** if storing in below-freezing conditions. Fill with the appropriate concentration of antifreeze (see engine operator's manual). The anti-freeze decal (Part 3102065-E) on the machine indicates the correct fill specification.  
     
2. **Top off the hydraulic reservoir** to the sight-eye level to minimize condensation in the tank during storage.  
     
3. **Run the engine to warm** all hydraulic circuits before shutdown, ensuring all moisture is dispersed through the system.  
     
4. **Change engine oil and filter** before long-term storage — contaminated oil during storage accelerates corrosion.  
     
5. **Fully retract all hydraulic cylinders** (gate cylinders, hitch cylinder) to protect cylinder rods from corrosion.  
     
6. **Protect all exposed metal surfaces** with an appropriate corrosion inhibitor.  
     
7. **Disconnect the battery** (or install a battery tender) if storing for more than 30 days to prevent battery discharge. Note: always place the Park/Drive switch in PARK before disconnecting power to avoid draining batteries accidentally.  
     
8. **Chock all four wheels** and engage the parking brake before leaving unattended.  
     
9. **Install the locking control box cover** to protect controls from weather and vandalism.  
     
10. **Check tire pressure** before storage and re-check monthly — cold temperatures lower tire pressure.  
      
11. **Upon return to service:** Follow the Hydrostatic System Startup procedure in Appendix D of M-213-16 if the hydraulic system was opened for any maintenance during storage.
