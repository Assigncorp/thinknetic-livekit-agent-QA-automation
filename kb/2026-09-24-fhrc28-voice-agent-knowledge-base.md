## Section 3 — Product Overview

The Etnyre Fixed Hopper ChipSpreader with RC28 is a self-propelled, computer-controlled hydrostatic chip-spreading machine designed to apply aggregate (chips) to freshly oiled road surfaces with precision application rates. It is engineered to improve the accuracy of chip spreading while improving productivity and simplifying operation through Application Rate capabilities using precise gate opening control and speed feedback in closed loop controls.

**Key Systems:**

- **Drive System:** Hydrostatic ground drive using a Rexroth AA4VG125 main drive pump. Available in 2WD (single front axle drive) or 4WD configuration with front and rear hydraulic motors (160 cc displacement).  
- **Controller:** RC-28 (Rexroth ECU computer), computerized closed-loop control of speed, gate opening, and application rate.  
- **Hopper:** Single fixed-width front hopper with hydraulic cylinder gate actuation. Individual air-operated gates controlled by solenoids. Gate opening range: standard 4.00 inches maximum (5.00 inches on big chipper models).  
- **Spreadroll:** Hydraulically driven at a target speed of 100 RPM, controlled by a flow control valve.  
- **Conveyors:** Two independently controlled rear conveyors (left and right) feed material from the supply truck into the front hopper. Speed adjustable from 10% to 99% (65% being the optimal speed for a continuous operation).  
- **Augers:** Two independently controlled augers distribute material in the front hopper.  
- **Hydraulic System:** Three hydraulic pumps — (1) main hydrostatic drive pump (Rexroth AA4VG125), (2) auxiliary pressure-compensating pump (Rexroth A10VO74) supplying conveyors/spreadroll/gates/hitch, (3) fixed displacement gear pump supplying power steering and fan.  
- **Air System:** Compressor, reservoir, oiler, water separator, main regulator (set 80 PSI), controls individual gate air cylinders.  
- **Hitch:** Hydraulic hitch with automatic latch, height adjustment, and release pushbutton. Optional traction boost cylinder.  
- **Operator Station:** Full control console with joystick (speed/direction), RC-28 display, all gate controls, conveyor controls, engine diagnostics, and optional sliding seat.

## Section 5 — Material Loading and Unloading

### Material Flow — Valve Positions

The hydraulic suction gate valve (located in the suction tube to the pumps) must always be in the correct position before operating.

| Valve / Component | Normal Operation | Startup After Maintenance | Storage / Shutdown |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Hydraulic Suction Gate Valve | Open (counterclockwise, sleeve and handle installed) | Open — must be fully open before cranking | CLOSED only during pump maintenance |
| Left Conveyor Selector | On / AUTO / Off per need | Off | Off |
| Right Conveyor Selector | On / AUTO / Off per need | Off | Off |
| Gate/Spreadroll Switch (Thumb Switch) | Off until at start line | Off | Off |
| Gate Master Switch | On for full-width spreading | On | Off |
| Individual Gate Switches (Left/Right) | On for full-width | As required | Off |
| Rear Hopper Flow Gate | Open to deliver material to conveyor | — | Closed |

### Conveyor Operating Modes

| Mode | Selector Position | Behavior |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Manual On | Bottom position | Conveyor runs continuously |
| Auto | Middle position | Conveyor starts when hopper material sensor detects empty, stops when full |
| Off | Upper position | Conveyor stopped |

**Note:** When operating with the truck, the rear conveyor gates should be set to deliver as much material as possible into the conveyor without spillage, then the conveyor speed adjusted to deliver slightly more aggregate to the front hopper than the amount being spread. When properly adjusted, conveyors should run approximately 80% of the time with the hopper spreading full width at maximum job speed.

**Agitator:** The front hopper agitator can be disengaged for clean, dry aggregate by removing the agitator disconnect bolt. Re-engage the agitator when spreading sand or small aggregate (bridging risk) by reinstalling the bolt. An optional second agitator is available for sand applications.

## Section 6 — System Startup

### Pre-Start Inspection Checklist

Before starting the engine each day, complete the following checks:

1. Inspect tires for proper pressure: **55–60 PSI front, 60–65 PSI rear**. Never exceed maximum inflation pressure shown on the tire sidewall.  
2. Check for fluid leaks (hydraulic oil, engine oil, coolant, fuel) at all visible hoses, fittings, and connections.  
3. Check hydraulic reservoir oil level at the sight eye on the tank.  
4. Check engine coolant level (refer to engine operator's manual).  
5. Check engine oil level (refer to engine operator's manual).  
6. Check fuel level — refuel if below 25% to avoid running the fuel sender alarm (alarm activates at approximately 10% / 7 gallons).  
7. Grease all lubrication fittings in accordance with the Chipspreader Lubrication Chart on the side of the tool box.  
8. Verify all shields, covers, and guards are in place.  
9. Confirm the control joystick is in the **Neutral** position.  
10. Confirm the Park/Drive switch is in the **Park** position.  
11. Verify wheels are chocked if machine is on a grade.  
12. Confirm the emergency stop switch is reset (twist clockwise if it was previously activated).  
13. Check hydraulic suction gate valve is **fully open** (counterclockwise).

>   
> **CAUTION:** Always start and operate the engine in a well-ventilated area. If in an enclosed area, vent the exhaust to the outside. Do not modify or tamper with the exhaust system.  
>   
> **WARNING:** Diesel engine exhaust and some of its constituents are known to the State of California to cause cancer, birth defects, and other reproductive harm. (Proposition 65\)

### Engine Startup Sequence

1. Place the **Park/Drive switch** in the **Park** position.  
     
2. Verify the **control joystick** is in the **neutral** (center) position.  
     
3. Place the **Throttle Run/Idle switch** in the **Idle** position.  
     
4. Rotate the **Power/Ignition switch** from Off to On — all system power energizes.  
     
5. Wait for the **"Wait To Start"** indicator to clear on the engine display (if Tier 3/4/5 diesel equipped).  
     
6. Rotate the ignition switch further right (spring-loaded) to the **Start** position to engage the starter. Release the key once the engine fires — the switch returns to On.  
     
   > **CAUTION:** Do not crank for more than 10 seconds at a time. Wait at least 2 minutes before attempting again.  
   >   
   > **NOTE:** The engine will not start with the Park/Drive switch in the Drive position. If started without the joystick in neutral, the machine will not move until the joystick is returned to neutral.  
     
7. Allow engine to warm up at **low idle** for a few minutes.  
     
8. Once warmed up, switch **Throttle Run/Idle** to the **Run** position. The engine will run at high idle. The hydrostatic system is designed to run at high idle for all work.  
     
9. Check the **computer display screen** — the main operator screen will show application rate set point, aggregate size, and speed set point.  
     
10. Confirm **charge pressure** is approximately 400 PSI (visible in service/setup screens if needed).  
      
11. Move the **Park/Drive switch** to **Drive** to enable machine movement.

>   
> **CAUTION:** Auxiliary computer power is routed through the Park/Drive switch. When the engine is not running, place the switch in the Park position to avoid draining the batteries.  
>   
> **WARNING:** Turning the ignition switch to "Off" results in a violent stop. Shut machine Off and wait for all movement to stop before leaving the operator's seat or servicing.

## Section 7 — Operating Procedures

### Normal Chip Spreading Work Cycle

1. **Hook Up to Supply Truck:** Back the chipspreader into the truck; the hitch automatically closes and locks on contact. Place the truck gearshift in neutral as soon as the truck is connected.  
     
2. **Set Aggregate Size:** Press the **Aggregate Size Switch** (4) to select the aggregate being spread (Sand, 1/4", 3/8" Chips, 3/8" Gravel, 5/8" Chips, or 1" Chips). This must be set before adjusting application rate or calibrating.  
     
3. **Set Application Rate:** Use the **Application Rate Switch** (5) to set the desired lbs/yd² set point. Pressing up increases the rate; pressing down decreases it.  
     
4. **Set Travel Speed:** With the joystick in neutral and the machine stopped, use the **Speed Set Toggle Switch** (3) to set the desired FPM (feet per minute). Pressing up increases the speed set point; pressing down decreases it.  
     
5. **Load Memory (Optional):** If this combination of rate/size/speed has been saved to a memory preset, press the appropriate **Memory Selector Button** (1–5) to restore all parameters at once.  
     
6. **Verify Gates Off:** Confirm the **Gate/Spreadroll Thumb Switch** (10) is in the Off (left) position.  
     
7. **Start Conveyors:** Set the Left and Right Conveyor Selector switches to AUTO or On to begin feeding material from the supply truck into the front hopper. Monitor the hopper level — when properly filled, conveyors should cycle on approximately 80% of the time.  
     
8. **Move to Starting Line:** Push the **joystick (9)** smoothly to the full forward position. The machine will accelerate to the set speed. Use the thumb switch detent and adjustable friction to hold desired positions.  
     
   > **WARNING:** Stay Off hopper while machine is moving. Machine movements could cause a fall resulting in injury or death.  
     
9. **Activate Gates at Starting Line:** Approximately 10 feet before the start line, depress the RIGHT side of the **Gate/Spreadroll Thumb Switch** (10) to turn gates On. The spreadroll also activates. The computer opens the gate to the position required for the selected speed and application rate.  
     
10. **Spread Material:** Drive at constant speed. The computer continuously adjusts the gate opening to maintain the selected application rate as speed changes.  
      
    - To change speed while spreading: use the **Speed Toggle Switch** — the chipspreader transitions smoothly to the new speed while maintaining the application rate.  
    - To momentarily open gates wider (to clear a jam): press and hold the **Gate Override Switch** (23). Gates return to set position when released.  
    - To turn Off individual gate sections: use the **Left/Right Gate Selector Switches** (43, 44) during operation.

    

11. **Close Gates at End Line:** Depress the Left (center) side of the **Gate/Spreadroll Thumb Switch** (10) to close the gates. The spreadroll deactivates.  
      
12. **Stop Machine:** Return the joystick to neutral. The parking brake is automatically applied once the speed sensor reads 0 FPM.  
      
13. **Release from Truck (if needed):** Push the **Hitch Release Pushbutton** (17 or 50) while momentarily pulling the joystick slightly rearward to create slack in the hitch. Push joystick forward again while holding hitch release. Once the truck separates, release the pushbutton.  
      
14. **Park Machine:** Place the **Park/Drive switch** in Park. Always install the locking control box cover and chock wheels when leaving the machine unattended.

### Speed Behavior Notes

- Gates automatically close when chipspreader speed falls below **30 FPM**, even if the thumb switch remains on. Gates will immediately begin to reopen when speed exceeds 30 FPM again.  
- Full reverse speed requires moving the joystick to the full reverse position. Max reverse speed is up to **1,300 FPM**.  
- After backing, reselect the memory button to restore your forward chipping speed.

### Conveyor Hood Adjustment

Conveyor hoods should be adjusted to control material distribution in the front hopper. A 1.5" gap is a good starting point for 3/8" to 1/2" chips. Generally, the larger the stone, the further forward the hood should be positioned.

### Towing Instructions

- Install a chain or strap around the front hydrostatic motor and parking brake assembly and secure the assembly to the front engine crossmember.
- Remove the four 16 mm bolts that secure the parking brake to the front axle, and pull the parking brake and motor assembly away from the front axle to disengage the spline.
- On a 4WD machine, secure the rear motor to the frame crossmember and remove the motor in the same manner.
- The Chipspreader should be towed to an area where it can be loaded onto a trailer. It is not recommended to tow the Chipspreader for long distances.
- When the Chipspreader is placed back in service, verify that the axle differential housings are filled with gear lube to the full level.

### Automatic Conveyor Control
- When the Left Conveyor Selector and/or the Right Conveyor Selector are in the middle position, power is supplied to the auto switch mounted on the outside of the spread hopper. When this switch senses material, the respective conveyor shuts "off", and when it no longer senses material, it starts the conveyor to fill the hopper.

### Operating Guidelines
- Should a piece of foreign material become lodged in the gates, push the gate override switch (Ref 23) to open the gate above the set point to allow the piece to pass. Releasing the override returns the gates to the previously set position.
- Adjust the Chipspreader hitch height as necessary to accommodate different individual trucks.
- Operate the conveyor belt switches to maintain an even distribution of aggregate in the front hopper.
- Use the conveyor flow deflectors to achieve the desired material distribution in the front hopper.
- Use the conveyor hoods to adjust the distribution to the front hopper, primarily to control the amount of material in the front hopper in the area in front of the conveyor. The amount of material in front of the conveyor affects when the auto conveyor switch is tripped to shut off the conveyor. Generally, the larger the stone, the further forward the hood should be positioned. Approximately a 1-1/2" gap is a good starting point for 3/8" to 1/2" chips.
- The machine is equipped with conveyor belt speed controls. The rear conveyor gates should be set to deliver as much material as possible into the conveyor without spillage, and then the conveyor speed should be adjusted to deliver slightly more aggregate to the front hopper than the amount being spread. When properly adjusted, the conveyors should run approximately 80% of the time with the hopper spreading full width and the Chipspreader traveling at maximum speed for the particular job.
- The operator may wish to disengage the front hopper agitator while spreading clean, dry aggregate. This is done by removing the agitator disconnect bolt, and prevents unnecessary wear of the agitator.

## Section 8 — Control Panels and RC-28 Controller

### Main Console Controls

#### 1. Power/Ignition Switch
- Rotating the switch from "off" to "on" supplies electric power to all systems and controls.
- Rotating the switch further to the right against the spring engages the starter. When the engine runs, release the key and the switch will remain in the "on" position.
- The engine will not start with the Park/Drive switch in the Drive position.
- It is recommended that the control joystick be in the neutral position during startup. If started without the control joystick in neutral, the Chipspreader will not move until the joystick has been returned to the neutral position.
- Since all functions except power steering and rear brakes are electrically controlled, turning the key to "off" results in a violent stop.

**Warning:** Turning the ignition switch to "off" results in a violent stop.

**Shut the machine off and wait for all movement to stop before leaving the operator's seat or servicing. Failure to do so could result in unexpected movement and cause serious injury or death.**

#### 2. Mode Selector — Drive/Park
A two-position switch selects either "Drive" or "Park".
- **Park:** The speed/direction handle (9) is disabled and the parking brake is applied. The parking brake is applied when the Chipspreader is stopped by returning the speed/direction handle to neutral, regardless of the position of the Drive/Park switch.
- **Drive:** The parking brake releases when the speed/direction handle (9) is moved from the neutral position, resulting in forward or reverse motion of the Chipspreader.
- Since engaging Park disables the speed/direction handle and applies the parking brake, selecting Park while the Chipspreader is moving will result in a violent stop. Do not select Park unless the Chipspreader is at a full stop.
- Auxiliary computer power is routed through the Park/Drive switch. When the engine is not running, place the switch in the Park position to avoid draining the batteries.
- The Park/Drive switch must be in the Park position after the computer has been turned on for the Chipspreader to be movable. If the computer is reset with the Park/Drive switch in Drive, it must be moved to Park first and then back to Drive to resume normal operation. Similarly, the control joystick must be returned to the neutral position after a computer restart to resume normal operation.

**Warning:** Selecting "Park" while the Chipspreader is moving results in an emergency stop.

**Caution:** Always place the mode selector switch in the "Park" position when the Chipspreader is stopped to avoid accidental movement of the machine.

#### 3. Speed Set Toggle Switch
- When in the "Drive" position, this switch provides the command signal (set point) to the computer. It is used to set the desired Chipspreader speed in feet per minute (FPM).
- Pushing the toggle switch up increases the speed set point. Pushing the switch down decreases the speed set point.
- The computer display screen shows the set point until the control handle is moved out of neutral, and then it shows the actual speed.
- Once the speed is set, the Chipspreader will repeat that speed any time the control handle is pushed fully forward.
- The switch can be pushed to increase or decrease the set point while the Chipspreader is moving. When this is done, the Chipspreader will smoothly transition to the new set point, and the display will show the set point rather than the actual speed. When the switch is released, the display switches back to the actual speed.
- If the engine cannot maintain the Chipspreader's speed on a steep hill, bring the control handle rearward toward neutral to slow the Chipspreader down so that the engine can recover to high idle RPM. When the steepness of the hill decreases, or the truck lightens up, push the control handle fully forward again to return to the set speed.

#### 4. Aggregate Size Switch
- This switch selects the size of the aggregate to be spread. The size should be set to the aggregate being spread before setting the application rate and before doing any calibrating of material.
- The choices of aggregate size are: Sand, 1/4" Chips, 3/8" Chips, 3/8" Gravel, 5/8" Chips, and 1" Chips.
- The amount of aggregate applied on the ground (application rate) is a function of the front hopper gate opening. The gate opening is determined by the selected aggregate (for example, 3/8 chips) and the Chipspreader speed. The computer controls the gate opening to maintain the application rate (for example, 20.0 lbs/yd²).

#### 5. Application Rate Switch
- This switch sets the position to which the gates will open by increasing or decreasing the set point. The set point is the application rate, in lbs/yd², that will be delivered when the Gate/Spreadroll switch (10) is activated.
- Pushing the switch up increases the set point. Pushing the switch down decreases the set point.
- The set point can be changed at any time, whether spreading aggregate or not.

#### 6. Circuit Breakers
These circuit breakers are powered from the accessory post on the ignition switch and lose voltage when the starter is engaged:
- **Light circuit breaker:** Supplies power to all lights except brake lights.
- **Conveyor circuit breaker:** Supplies power to the conveyor and auger switches.
- **Controller circuit breaker:** Supplies power to the joystick, display, hitch functions, hopper functions, seat position, batwings, and gate override.

These circuit breakers are powered from the ignition post on the ignition switch and maintain voltage while the starter is engaged:
- **Horn circuit breaker:** Supplies power to the horn, input switches, and front control box.
- **Gate circuit breaker:** Supplies power to the gate power switches, the spreadroll power switches, and the individual gates when required.
- **Motor circuit breaker:** Supplies power to the brake lights, speed sensor, and traction control switch.

The computer is fused with an in-line 30 amp fuse and is powered from the accessory post on the ignition switch.

#### 7. Computer Display Screens

**Operator Screen** (example: `20.0 lbs/yd2 | 3/8 Chip | 400 fpm`)
- This screen is displayed when power is turned on to the computer. It shows the application rate set point, aggregate size, and speed set point while at a standstill. These values can all be changed as described in earlier sections.
- When the Chipspreader is powered down, the values currently on the screen are saved and returned the next time power is turned on.
- The speed set point is displayed while the Chipspreader is not moving. Once the Chipspreader is in motion, the actual speed is displayed. The speed feedback is generated by a magnetic pickup mounted on the motor output shaft, which generates pulses that the computer displays as feet per minute (FPM).

**Memory Presets**
- Different configurations of these three values can be saved in one of five memory presets. The memory presets store an application rate set point, an aggregate, and a speed set point in one of the memory locations. The memory pushbutton then restores the information stored in that location to the screen when activated. This allows the operator to switch between stored combinations with the push of a button instead of scrolling the individual values.
- The memory functions simply restore the selected values to the operator's screen. If one of the values is changed while operating, the values stored in the memory will not change. Pressing the memory pushbutton will restore the saved values and overwrite the changed speed set point.
- **To save information in a memory:** adjust the application rate set point, aggregate size, and speed set point to values that will be commonly used together. When the values displayed on the screen are set to the desired values, press the save pushbutton. The display will change to read "Select location 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5". Select the memory location by pressing the corresponding memory pushbutton (for example, memory 1). The display will return to the main operator screen.
- **To check the save:** press the memory pushbutton the information was saved to. The display will read "Restoring memory 1" and will then show the values saved in memory 1.
- Certain material conditions could require a calibration of the computer to ensure the application rate set point is equal to the actual application on the ground. It is the aggregate that is calibrated, not the memory. For example, if 3/8 chips are saved in memory 1 with an application rate set point of 20.0 lb/yd² and also in memory 2 with an application rate of 8 lb/yd², a calibration of 3/8 chips will update both memory locations. The memory functions serve as a means to quickly change between frequently used operating parameters.
- If the set point on the display is dramatically different from the application on the ground, it is possible the incorrect aggregate has been selected. If that is not the case, use the following procedure to calibrate the aggregate and correct the application rate information stored in the computer.

**Conveyor Speed Screen** (example: `Left Conv 0% | Right Conv 0%`)
- This screen shows how fast the conveyors are moving. The speed of the conveyors can be varied independently to provide a uniform distribution of material to the front hopper. With the percentage set to 99%, the conveyor runs at its highest speed. With the percentage set to 10%, the conveyor runs at its slowest speed.
- To change the speed of the conveyors, press the CAL switch up or down. The screen will show the set percentage of the left conveyor (example: `Left Conv 85%`).
- Pressing the cal switch up (+) increases the left conveyor speed by 5%. Pressing the cal switch down (-) decreases it by 5%. Pressing the scroll switch down (-) shows the screen for changing the right conveyor speed (example: `Right Conv 85%`).
- Pressing the cal switch up (+) increases the right conveyor speed by 5%. Pressing the cal switch down (-) decreases it by 5%. Pressing the scroll switch up (+) shows the screen for changing the left conveyor speed. Pressing the scroll switch up (+) again shows the actual speed of both conveyors.
- The conveyor speed percentage can be changed at any time, whether or not the conveyors are moving.
- Adjusting the speed of the conveyors is particularly useful for shoulder work or operations requiring less than full hopper width. It is also useful for smoothing out delivery of material to match the rate being spread. When properly adjusted, the conveyors should run approximately 80% of the time with the hopper at maximum width and the Chipspreader traveling at maximum speed for the particular job.
- To move to the next screen, press the scroll switch down (-).

**Auger Speed Screen** (example: `Left Auger 0% | Right Auger 0%`)
- This screen shows how fast the augers are moving. The speed of the augers can be varied independently to provide a uniform distribution of material in the front hopper. With the percentage set to 99%, the auger runs at its highest speed. With the percentage set to 10%, the auger runs at its slowest speed.
- To change the speed of the augers, press the CAL switch up or down. The screen will show the set percentage of the left auger (example: `Left Auger 85%`).
- Pressing the cal switch up (+) increases the left auger speed by 5%. Pressing the cal switch down (-) decreases it by 5%. Pressing the scroll switch down (-) shows the screen for changing the right auger speed (example: `Right Auger 85%`).
- Pressing the cal switch up (+) increases the right auger speed by 5%. Pressing the cal switch down (-) decreases it by 5%. Pressing the scroll switch up (+) shows the screen for changing the left auger speed. Pressing the scroll switch up (+) again shows the actual speed of both augers.
- The auger speed percentage can be changed at any time, whether or not the augers are moving.
- To move to the next screen, press the scroll switch down (-).

**Water / Oil / Fuel Screen** (example: `100°F WATER | 60 PSI OIL | 15% FUEL`)
- This screen shows the engine coolant temperature on the left, the engine oil pressure in the center, and the fuel remaining in the fuel tank on the right.
- To move to the next screen, push the scroll switch down (-).

**Hydraulic Oil / Engine / Battery Screen** (example: `100°F HYD OIL | 2200 RPM ENGINE | 13.8 V BATTERY`)
- This screen shows the hydraulic oil temperature on the left, the engine RPM in the center, and the system voltage on the right.
- The right side shows battery voltage when the ignition is on but the engine is not running. The voltage will increase to 13.5 to 15.0 volts as the output of the alternator supplies voltage to the system.
- To move to the next screen, push the scroll switch down (-).

#### 9. Speed/Direction Control Handle (Joystick)
- The control handle controls both direction (forward, neutral, reverse) and rate of speed.
- Full reverse movement gives 100% of the preset speed in reverse, up to 1,300 FPM. To back up faster, you must increase the speed setting. After backing up, you must reselect the appropriate memory button to reset the speed to your chipping speed.
- Full forward movement gives 100% of the selected speed set point.
- Speed can be slowed down at any time by pulling the handle back toward neutral, and resumed by pushing the handle fully forward at any time.
- The control handle controls the rate of acceleration and deceleration. If the handle is moved quickly, the Chipspreader responds quickly. If the handle is moved slowly, the Chipspreader responds slowly.
- The control handle also incorporates a neutral safety start switch, automatic application of the parking brake and brake lights when placed in neutral, and activation of the backup alarm. The handle has a detent in the neutral position and an adjustable friction drag for holding at any desired position other than neutral.
- Decelerating is always accomplished by moving the handle toward neutral. Upon reaching the neutral position, the parking brake is automatically applied after the speed sensor reads "0" speed.
- Moving the handle rearward out of the neutral position approximately 3 degrees activates the backup alarm and releases the brakes. Moving the handle further rearward increases the reverse speed in proportion to the handle movement until full set speed is reached with the handle in the full reverse position.
- Moving the handle forward out of the neutral position approximately 3 degrees releases the brakes and begins to increase the forward speed, until at the full forward position of the handle the Chipspreader is at 100% of the set point speed and the computer maintains the set speed. If the set speed is 200 FPM, the handle must be moved fully forward to obtain 200 FPM. If the set speed is 500 FPM, the handle must be fully forward to reach 500 FPM.
- The rate of acceleration to the set speed is always controlled by how fast the handle is moved from neutral to the full forward position.
- Adjustments to the set point speed are made using the "Speed" toggle switch, and can be made at any time. The speed may be decreased at any time by moving the handle rearward toward neutral without changing the set point, and then brought back up to the set point by moving the handle fully forward again. The aggregate application rate is automatically maintained when either of these methods is used to adjust the travel speed.
- **Stopping is always accomplished by returning the handle to neutral.**

## Section 9 — Calibration

### Why Calibrate?

Material calibration ensures that the application rate displayed on the screen (lbs/yd²) equals what is actually being applied to the ground. Aggregate density, moisture content, and particle shape vary — a fresh calibration is needed whenever material changes, when the application rate on the ground differs significantly from the set point, or after replacing a gate transducer.

### When to Calibrate

- When starting a new job with a different material  
- When the actual spread rate on the ground differs from the set point by more than 2 lbs/yd²  
- After adjusting or replacing the gate transducer  
- When changing aggregate size on the display

### Material Calibration — Step-by-Step

**Before starting:** Verify the spreadroll is turning at 100 RPM and ensure air gates are adjusted properly and uniform with each other. Select the correct aggregate size on the display. Set the application rate set point to the desired value. Set speed set point to approximately 300 FPM. Have the canvas (supplied with machine) and scale ready.

1. Place the canvas on a flat surface with room for the chipspreader to get up to speed.  
2. With the joystick pushed fully forward, maneuver toward the canvas at constant speed.  
3. Activate the gate thumb switch approximately **10 feet before** the material will hit the canvas — this ensures the gate is in the correct position.  
4. Once the canvas is covered, deactivate the gate thumb switch.  
5. Weigh the material and canvas together. Then weigh the canvas empty. Subtract to get the pounds per square yard of material on the canvas.  
6. Repeat a **minimum of 3 times** and average the values before making any changes.

**Entering Calibration into RC-28:**

This Fixed Hopper Chipspreader uses the **Left Hopper calibration screens** (the program is universal; the fixed hopper uses left hopper screens).

7. Press the CAL switch UP or Down to access calibration screens. The screen will show:  
     
   0.0       95.6% lb/yd²    Right CAL  
     
8. Press the **Scroll switch Down** to advance past the right hopper screen to the left hopper:  
     
   0.0       95.6% lb/yd²    Left CAL  
     
9. Example: If the hopper weighed 23.5 lbs with a 20.0 lbs/yd² set point — measured is 3.5 lbs too heavy. Press and hold the CAL switch Down until the lb/yd² display reads **\-3.5**.  
     
   - If material is lighter than desired: press CAL switch UP to enter a positive value.  
   - The Left CAL percentage value is a reference density factor.

   

10. Press the **Save pushbutton** to store the calibration. This immediately decreases/increases gate output by the entered amount. The screen resets to zero once saved.  
      
11. Press the Scroll switch Up **twice** to return to the main operator screen.

**Fine Tuning While Chipping:**

- If the spread appears lighter than desired: press CAL, scroll to Left CAL screen, toggle CAL UP to raise the value 1–2 lbs, press Save. Change takes effect immediately.  
- Calibration is permanent — values are retained when the machine is powered down.

### Gate Transducer Calibration (Left Gate — Fixed Hopper)

#### Setting the Gates Using the Computer Set Up Screens
1. Place the Drive/Park selector in "PARK".
2. Hold the CAL switch down and start the engine. Release the CAL switch once the computer beeps three times. This will access the computer set up screens.
3. Scroll down until the display reads Left Gate Null. Note that on a fixed hopper the right gate is not used. This value should be around 0.0. If it is not, verify that the gate is closed. There should be 1/16" between each gate and the spreadroll. Once the gate is shut, press the CAL switch down to set the closed value (null).
4. Once the null is set, scroll down to the next screen, Left Gate Scale. Hold the gate override pushbutton down and monitor the display. The display should show 4.00" while the override is activated. If it does not, verify that the gate is fully open. Once the gate is fully opened, press the CAL switch down while holding the override to set the opening to 4.00". When the gate override is released, the display should go back to 0.00".

It is recommended to try to set the gates using the computer set up screens prior to physically making any adjustments to the potentiometer itself. If the gates cannot be set using the set up screens, or a new gate potentiometer is to be installed, use the following procedure.

#### Adjusting or Installing the Gate Potentiometer (Transducer)
1. Place the Drive/Park selector in "Park".
2. Start the engine and run it at about 1000 RPM.
3. Check that the gate switch is turned off and the gates appear to be closed, and then shut the engine off.
4. To ensure that the gates are fully closed, swap the connectors on the gate open and close valve and restart the engine. Depress the gate override button, which will fully close the gates. While holding the override button down, shut off the engine. There should be 1/16" between each gate and the spreadroll.
5. Replace the connectors in their correct positions.
6. Enter the set up access screens by holding the "Cal" switch down while turning the key on. Do not start the engine. Scroll down to the left gate null setting, and set the null to zero by depressing the "Cal" switch. The screen should change to 0.0. Then scroll down to the save screen and save the value.
7. Using needle probes on a digital voltmeter, measure the voltage between the red and black wires at the gate transducer. The value should be 5.0 volts DC. Next, check the voltage between the blue and black wires. This value should be between 0.5 and 1.5 volts DC.
8. If the value measured in step 7 is not within the specified tolerance, remove the transducer cover on the hopper.
9. Loosen the transducer mounting bolts and rotate the transducer as necessary to get the 0.5–1.5 volt DC reading.
10. Retighten the mounting bolts.
11. Recheck the reading.
12. Start the engine and depress the gate override button to fully open the gates. While depressing the override button, shut the engine off. The gates should remain fully open. Turn the key back on and measure the voltage between the blue and black wires. The voltage should be 3.5 to 4.5 volts DC. Restart the engine, let the gates close, and recheck that the closed value is still within the specified range of 0.5–1.5 volts. If it is, enter the set up screens while starting the engine and scroll down to the appropriate gate scale screen.
13. Depress the override button to fully open the gates and then depress the Cal switch. The reading should change to 4.00.
14. Release the override button and scroll up to the "Gate Null" screen to check the reading. If it is no longer at "0", depress the override button and scroll down to the "Gate Scale" screen. Depress the override button, and while depressing the override button, depress the "Cal" button to set the scale.
15. Repeat these two steps as required until the readings get to "0" and "4", or until they no longer change.
16. Upon completion, scroll to the save screen and exit set up by depressing the "Save" button.

### Pressure Settings for Hydrostatic Drive Pump (Rexroth AA4VG125 Pump)

The pressure settings consist of two (2) high pressure cross-port relief valves (forward and reverse) and the pressure override (pressure cut-off) for the AA4VG125 pump. The adjustment procedure is as follows:

1. **Install pressure gauges.** Install 10,000 PSI pressure gauges in ports MA and MB, located on the top side of the pump. These ports are -04 SAE O-ring. The pressure gauges should be installed with enough hose to see the gauge without getting under the machine.

2. **Turn the pressure override (POR) all the way in.** The pressure override (POR) should be turned all the way in to be able to obtain the highest possible pressure cut-off setting. Turn the screw in (clockwise) until resistance is encountered. Do not force the adjustment past this point.
   - The POR adjustment is located below the curb side high pressure port.
   - The adjustment requires a 4mm allen wrench and a 13mm box wrench.
   - The protective plastic cover may have to be removed to gain access to the POR adjustment.

3. **Set the forward and reverse high pressure cross-port relief valves (Relief Valves A & B) to approximately 7,000 PSI.**
   - First, disable the parking brake release circuit by disconnecting the Weatherpack connector at the parking brake release solenoid. This solenoid is located on the hydraulic manifold under the center deck cover below the operator's station. The brake release solenoid is the forward-most coil towards the curb side of the machine. Disabling this circuit will ensure the Chipspreader will not move while checking pressures.
   - On 4WD units, the rear hoses must be capped to prevent drive hoses at the drive pump and install 16MB caps on the adapters and 16MB plugs in the hose ends.
   - Once the parking brake release has been disabled, unhook the brake pressure switch, if equipped. The switch is located on the hose coming from the foot pedal.
   - Apply the foot pedal brake and push the joystick forward, developing maximum system pressure. Check the reading on the gauge in port MA for forward high pressure.
   - Should the pressure need adjusting, you may have to remove the protective plastic cover located above the high pressure port on the street side of the pump. Turn the adjusting screw in (clockwise) to increase the relief setting. This adjustment requires a 5mm allen wrench and a 17mm box wrench.
   - Once the forward cross-port relief valve (Relief Valve A) has been set to 7,000 PSI, repeat the above procedure for reverse (Relief Valve B). The adjustment for this relief is located above the curb side high pressure port.

   **The correct procedure is to:**
   1. Put the pump on stroke and see where the relief valve pressure setting is, but do not leave the pump on stroke for more than a few seconds.
   2. Put the pump to neutral.
   3. Make an adjustment to the relief valve. Put the pump on stroke again and see where the pressure level is.

   Repeat the above process as many times as necessary until the correct pressure level is obtained.

4. **Set the Pressure Override Valve to 6,500 PSI.** Once the two high pressure relief valves are set correctly for forward and reverse, set the Pressure Override Valve to 6,500 PSI. To do this, adjust the POR screw out (counterclockwise) until the high pressure reads 6,500 PSI.
   - The POR adjustment is the same screw that was turned in fully in step 2.
   - There is only one setting for this relief, and it controls both forward and reverse.
   - Once the pressure override setting is below that of the two high pressure cross-port relief valves, the pump can be left on stroke without any problems, as there will be no flow across the high pressure cross-port relief valves.

5. **Remove the gauges.**

### Pressure Settings for Auxiliary Pump (Rexroth A10VO74 Pump)

- This pump is stacked on the back of the drive pump.
- On a fixed hopper Chipspreader, this pump supplies the flow for the left and right conveyors, the spreadroll, the gate cylinder, and the hitch functions.
- The pump has an internal high pressure relief setting and a standby (margin) pressure setting.
  - The high pressure relief should be set to 3,000 PSI.
  - The standby pressure should be set to 400 PSI.

### Setting the Charge Pressure

1. Install a 600 PSI gauge in the "CGP" port on the hitch manifold. The gauge will require a size 04 male boss end to plumb to the gauge port. With the engine at low idle, the charge pressure should read 400 PSI.

2. If the pressure is below 380 PSI or above 420 PSI, an adjustment should be made to the charge pressure relief valve.
   - To access the relief valve adjustment, you may have to remove the protective plastic cap located on the top side of the pump beside the port marked "G".
   - Using a 17mm box wrench with a 5mm allen wrench, loosen the jam nut and turn the set screw clockwise if the pressure is low.
   - If the pressure is high, loosen the jam nut and turn the set screw counterclockwise.
   - One turn is equal to 55 PSI.

3. Remove the gauge.

### Relief Valve Settings

1. Install a 1000 PSI gauge, with a shutoff valve, in the port marked "TP" on the conveyor manifold located on top of the hydraulic reservoir. This port is SAE 04 and requires a 9/16" wrench to remove the plug.

2. Disconnect the electrical connectors from the gate valve.
   - Make sure the left and right conveyor switches are in the "off" position and the thumb switch on the control handle is in the center position before starting the engine.
   - Make sure the shutoff valve is shut. Failure to do so could result in a blown gauge.
   - Start the engine, run it at low idle, and monitor the gauge pressure. The standby pressure should be 400 PSI.

3. To make an adjustment, use a 17mm wrench to remove the protective cap from the set screw.
   - The standby pressure adjustment is the higher of the two adjustments at the rear of the pump.
   - Loosen the jam nut on the rear pump and turn the set screw until the pressure reads 400 PSI on the gauge.
   - Replace the protective cover once the correct pressure is set. Tighten the jam nut. Shut the engine off.

4. Install a 5000 PSI gauge in the port marked "TP" on the conveyor manifold located on top of the hydraulic reservoir. Remove the pressure hose plumbed to the left conveyor at the manifold and cap the adapter in the port marked "A2" with a 1/2" (08) JIC hydraulic cap.

5. With the engine running at half throttle, turn on the left conveyor and monitor the gauge pressure. If the pressure reading is below 2950 PSI or above 3050 PSI, an adjustment should be made.

6. To make an adjustment, use a 17mm wrench to remove the protective cap from the relief set screw, located at the rear of the pump on the curb side of the Chipspreader.
   - Of the two adjustments at this location, the high pressure relief is the lower one.
   - If the pressure is low, loosen the jam nut and turn the set screw clockwise using a 5mm allen wrench.
   - If the pressure is high, loosen the jam nut and turn the set screw counterclockwise.
   - Replace the protective cover once the correct pressure is set.

7. Reinstall the hose removed in step 1 and remove the gauge.

8. Remove the gauge and reinstall the 1000 PSI gauge with the shutoff closed. Restart the engine and let it idle. Open the shutoff valve and verify that the standby pressure is still at 400 PSI, and adjust if necessary.

9. Reconnect the electrical connectors on the gate valve and remove the gauge.

### Pressure/Flow Settings for Actuator Valving

(Functions supplied by the pressure compensating auxiliary pump)

**Note:** Other than individual circuits that require reduced pressure for mechanical purposes, the main system relief is set at the pump.

### Gate Cylinder Relief Valve Setting

The relief setting for the gate cylinder relief valve should be set to 1500 PSI.

1. Install a 2000 PSI gauge in the "TP" port of the gate cylinder manifold, located just behind the conveyor manifold on the hydraulic reservoir. This port is SAE 04 MB and requires a 9/16" wrench to remove the plug.

2. With the engine at high idle, depress and hold the gate override pushbutton and monitor the pressure. If the pressure is below 1450 PSI or above 1550 PSI, an adjustment should be made.

3. To adjust the pressure, use a 9/16" box wrench to loosen the jam nut on the set screw located on the gate manifold on top of the reservoir.
   - If the pressure is low, turn the set screw clockwise.
   - If the pressure is high, turn the set screw counterclockwise.

4. Remove the gauge.

### Spreadroll Speed Adjustment

The spreadroll should be set at 100 RPM.

1. With the engine at high idle and the Park/Drive switch in the Park position, push the joystick slightly out of neutral and depress the right side of the thumb switch, engaging the gate/spreadroll circuit. Time the spreadroll with a stopwatch or a low speed tachometer to determine the revolutions per minute.

2. To adjust the speed, loosen the jam wheel on the spreadroll flow control valve, located between the conveyors.
   - Turn the adjuster knob clockwise to decrease the speed of the spreadroll.
   - Turn the adjuster knob counterclockwise to increase the speed of the spreadroll.

### Pressure Settings for Actuator Valving Supplied by Rear Auxiliary Pump

#### I. Main Relief Pressure (MPT)
The main relief pressure should be set to 1200 PSI. The steering motor operates at this pressure.

1. Install a 3000 PSI gauge in the port marked "MPT" on the hitch manifold, located under the center deck cover below the operator's station. This port is SAE 04 MB and requires a 1/4" allen wrench to remove the plug.

2. With the engine at or above 1000 RPM, press the batwing lower switch until it hits the stop. While holding the switch in the "lower" position, monitor the gauge pressure.

   **Note:** The engine must not be running to adjust the set screw. When the cap is removed, oil will flow through the valve and spray out the top of the valve. Shut the engine off before proceeding to make any adjustment.

3. If the pressure is low, remove the cap from the top of the relief valve using vise grips and adjust the set screw clockwise using a 1/4" allen wrench. If the pressure is high, adjust the set screw counterclockwise.

#### II. Pilot Pressure (PP)
The pilot pressure should be set to 250 PSI. The hitch raise/lower function operates at this pressure.

1. Install a 600 PSI gauge in the port marked "PP" on the steering/hitch manifold. This port is SAE 04 MB and requires a 1/4" allen wrench to remove the plug.

2. With the engine at or above 1000 RPM, monitor the pressure.

   **Note:** The engine must not be running to adjust the set screw. When the cap is removed, oil will flow through the valve and spray out the top of the valve. Shut the engine off before proceeding to make any adjustment.

3. If the pressure is low, remove the cap from the top of the relief valve using vise grips and adjust the set screw clockwise using a 1/4" allen wrench. If the pressure is high, adjust the set screw counterclockwise.

4. Remove the gauge.

#### III. Fan Valve Pressure Setting
The fan valve relief should be set to 2000 PSI. The fan motor operates at this pressure.

1. Remove the hose from the "M" port on the fan valve, located behind the cooler. Cap the end of the hose and install a 3000 PSI gauge into port "M" on the fan valve. This port is an SAE 16 and requires a 1-1/2" wrench for the hose and cap.

2. With the engine at high idle, unplug the connector at the fan valve and monitor the pressure. If the pressure is below 1900 PSI, an adjustment should be made.

3. To adjust the pressure, turn off the engine and adjust the relief set screw on the fan valve. A 9/16" box wrench is required to loosen the jam nut, and a 5/32" allen wrench is required to adjust the set screw.
   - If the pressure is low, turn the set screw clockwise.
   - If the pressure is high, turn the set screw counterclockwise.

4. Restart the engine and monitor the pressure. Repeat step 3 as necessary to adjust the pressure to the range of 1900 PSI to 2100 PSI.

5. Shut the engine off. Remove the gauge, reinstall the hose, and reconnect the electrical connection on the fan valve.

## Section 10 - Chipspreader Computer Set-Up Screens (Fixed Hopper Computer Set-Up)

### Computer Set Up
The computer must be set up and the various sensors calibrated for the particular Chipspreader that the computer is installed in. This is normally done at the factory, and the settings are retained in the computer's non-volatile memory. Normally an entire setup does not have to be done in the field, but if a joystick or a gate transducer is replaced, that particular item would have to be re-calibrated. In the case of a gate transducer, it should be mechanically set using the procedure described under "Hopper Gate Transducer Adjustment" before re-calibrating the computer.

## Section 12 — Troubleshooting — Machine Systems

All troubleshooting below is sourced from M-218-11R.

---

### Q: The display shows a speed sensor error, or the alarm sounds when the joystick is out of neutral but the machine does not move

**Step 1 — Joystick Neutral Check:** Before anything else, confirm the joystick is in the neutral (center) position. The alarm will sound if the joystick is out of neutral and the machine does not move. Return joystick to neutral; if alarm clears, the sensor was reading correctly and the issue may be resolved.

**Step 2 — Fuses and Circuit Breakers:** At the control console, check all fuses and circuit breakers. Locate the inline 30-amp computer fuse at the main fuse block (right rear area of machine). Replace any blown fuse with one of the same rating — never install a larger fuse.

**Step 3 — Physical Speed Sensor Check (at front motor, Spicer or Kessler axle):** Locate the magnetic pickup sensor on the front axle drive motor output (see Figure 1A/1B in M-218-11R). Check for: (a) loose sensor — sensor must be firmly mounted; (b) poor alignment — sensor must face the gear/pick-up target; (c) improper clearance — recommended gap is **1/16"** between sensor tip and gear; (d) loose wiring connection at sensor; (e) pinched or rubbed wiring along harness route.

**Step 4 — Wiring Check (harness disconnected from sensor):** Using a multimeter, measure at the sensor harness connector:

- Red wire: should read **12 VDC**  
- Black wire: **ground**  
- Clear or white wire (signal): should read **9–10 VDC** at rest  
- Shield: ground If voltages are OK, proceed to Step 5 (harness connected). If voltages are not OK, proceed to Step 6 (computer connector check).

**Step 5 — Sensor Output Check (harness connected, machine moving):**

- Red wire: **12 VDC**  
- Black wire: ground  
- Clear/white signal wire: **4–7 VAC while machine is moving** — if no signal, replace the speed sensor.  
  - Kessler axle speed sensor: Part No. **6703670**

**Step 6 — Computer Connector Check (sensor disconnected):**

- At computer connector P1-Pin 21: should read **12 VDC** (speed sensor power)  
  - If OK at computer but no voltage in Step 4 (red wire): check wiring harness continuity between computer and sensor. If no continuity: replace harness.  
  - If no voltage at P1-Pin 21: bad connection or bad computer output.  
- At computer connector P3-Pin 10: should read **9–10 VDC** (signal)  
  - If OK at P3-Pin 10 but no signal in Step 4 (clear wire): check harness continuity. Replace harness if no continuity.  
  - If NO voltage at P3-Pin 10: check voltage at other end of pull-up resistor on P3-Pin 10\. Should be 12 VDC. If 12 VDC is present at one end but not the other: replace the 1k OHM pull-up resistor.  
- Ground: attached to computer base/chassis.

### Q: The display shows a fuel alarm, or the fuel alarm keeps sounding even though I filled up

**Step 1 — Verify Fuel Level:** Check the actual fuel level physically or on the display screen (FUEL % on Screen 2). The alarm activates at approximately **10% / 7 gallons remaining** (usable). If the tank is genuinely low, add fuel and confirm alarm clears.

**Step 2 — Check for Debris on Sender:** Open the fuel tank access and visually inspect for metallic debris lying on top of the sending unit float. Debris can push the float down, falsely triggering the alarm. Remove any debris.

**Step 3 — Wiring Check:** Inspect the wiring from the fuel sending unit to the computer for loose connections, frayed wires, or bare wire touching ground (grounded wiring falsely triggers the low-fuel alarm).

**Step 4 — Sender Isolation Test:** Disconnect the wire from the fuel sending unit.

- If the alarm stops: the sender itself is faulty or out of resistance spec. Refer to the resistance-vs-gallons chart (Figure 2 in M-218-11R). Check sender resistance with a multimeter against the chart values.  
- If the alarm continues: the problem is in the wiring or computer — proceed to Step 5\.

**Step 5 — Wiring Between Sender and Computer:** Check continuity of wiring between the fuel sender and computer. Look for shorts, open circuits, or corroded connectors along the full harness length.

**Step 6 — Computer Connector Check:** Inspect computer connector for loose or corroded pins.

**Step 7 — Computer Terminal Check:** At computer terminal **P2-Pin 19**: verify proper voltage/resistance signal is arriving from the fuel sender. A bad reading here indicates wiring or sender fault. If signal is good but alarm persists, the computer may need service.

### Q: Low oil pressure alarm is on

**Step 1 — Verify with Mechanical Gauge:** Install a mechanical oil pressure gauge on the engine and verify actual engine oil pressure. Expected values:

- **\~30 PSI at idle**  
- **\~65 PSI at full RPM** (engine coolant at operating temperature 160–190°F) If mechanical pressure is low, the engine has an actual oil pressure problem — stop the engine immediately and investigate engine oil level, oil pump, and oil system.

**Step 2 — Sensor Wiring Check:** Inspect wiring at the oil pressure sensor (on engine block) for bad or loose connection. Repair or tighten as needed.

**Step 3 — Sensor Ground Test:** Ground the sensor signal wire directly to the engine block.

- If the alarm stops: the sender is out of resistance specification. Reference the resistance chart (Figure 3 in M-218-11R) and install a new OEM oil pressure sender with correct resistance values.  
  - Alarm triggers at: **5 PSI @ 1400 RPM and below** / **10 PSI @ 1401 RPM and above** (must exist for 2 seconds to activate alarm).  
- If the alarm continues: the problem is in the wiring or computer — proceed to Step 4\.

**Step 4 — Computer Connectors P2 and P3:** Inspect both connectors for loose pins, corrosion, or damage.

**Step 5 — Computer Terminal Check:**

- Oil pressure sender signal: **P2-Pin 18**  
- Oil pressure ground: **P2-Pin 2** Measure signal and ground at these pins. Bad readings indicate wiring fault between sensor and computer.

**Step 6 — Wiring Inspection:** Look for wires pulled out, touching other wires, or disconnected along the entire harness from engine sensor to computer.

**Step 7 — Continuity Check:** Check continuity of wiring between computer and oil pressure sender from P2-Pin 18 all the way to the sender connector.

**Step 8 — Replace Sender:** Install new oil pressure sender — must be OEM replacement with correct resistance values. Aftermarket senders with incorrect resistance will not work properly with the Rexroth computer.

### Q: Water temperature alarm is on / engine is overheating

**Step 1 — Verify with External Gauge:** Use a laser infrared probe or mechanical temperature gauge to verify the actual coolant temperature.

- If not exceeding 240°F: the sensor or wiring is falsely triggering the alarm — proceed to Step 2\.  
- If coolant IS exceeding 240°F: the engine is genuinely overheating — jump to Step 7\.

**Step 2 — Sensor Terminal Check:** Inspect the wiring terminal at the coolant temperature sensor (on engine) for a loose or bad connection. Tighten or repair.

**Step 3 — Wiring Inspection:** Check wiring for: (a) frayed wiring; (b) bare wire. Repair as needed.

**Step 4 — Sensor Isolation Test:** Disconnect the sensor.

- If alarm stops: the sender is faulty or out of resistance spec. Refer to resistance chart (Figure 4 in M-218-11R). Expected resistance decreases as temperature increases.  
- If alarm continues: problem is in wiring or computer — proceed to Step 5\.

**Step 5 — Computer Connection P2:** Check for loose or bad connection at computer connector P2.

**Step 6 — Computer Terminal:** Wiring from sensor to computer connects to **P2-Pin 20**.

**Step 7 — Coolant Level:** Carefully remove the radiator cap (use caution — engine HOT) and check coolant level. Add coolant if low. Low coolant is the most common cause of genuine overheating.

**Step 8 — Radiator Debris:** Check and clean any debris (stone chips, dust, mud) from the front of the radiator. Chip spreading generates significant debris accumulation.

**Step 9 — Cooler Plugging:** Inspect the radiator and hydraulic oil cooler for plugging with aggregate dust or particles. Clean if clogged — a plugged cooler severely reduces cooling capacity.

**Step 10 — Cooling System Leaks:** Check for leaks in the cooling system. Leaks allow particles to collect and block passages.

**Step 11 — Investigate Further:** If overheating continues after all above steps, investigate the radiator, water pump, thermostat, and engine internally.

### Q: Hydraulic oil temperature alarm is on

**Step 1 — Oil Level:** Check hydraulic oil level at the sight eye on the hydraulic reservoir. Add oil if low (see Lubrication chart for correct oil type).

**Step 2 — Verify Actual Temperature:** Use a laser probe or mechanical temp gauge at the hydraulic reservoir.

- If oil temperature IS above 180°F: genuine overheating — proceed to Step 3\.  
- If oil temperature is below 180°F: false alarm — skip to Step 9 (sensor check).

**Step 3 — Filter Restriction:** Check the filter restriction indicator gauges on the filter bases (both return filters). A damaged or broken gauge should be replaced. A blocked filter creates heat.

**Step 4 — Hydraulic Cooler Debris (front):** Check for stone chips, dust, or other debris trapped in front of the hydraulic oil cooler. Debris indicates a possible leak in the hydraulic or cooling system. Clean thoroughly.

**Step 5 — Between Coolers:** Check for debris trapped between the hydraulic cooler and the engine radiator — this blocks airflow between both coolers.

**Step 6 — Heat Source Identification:** Try to locate the source of heat generation: manifold blocks, quick couplings, hydraulic fittings, or hoses that are unusually hot to the touch (use caution).

**Step 7 — System Plumbing:** Verify the hydraulic system is plumbed correctly, especially that oil flow runs through the hydraulic cooler on return.

**Step 8 — Hydraulic Pressures:** Check hydraulic pressures to ensure they are not above allowable limits (see Section 2 — Specifications). Pressures above spec generate excessive heat.

**Step 9 — Temperature Sensor Connection:** Check the connection at the temperature sensor on the hydraulic reservoir tank for a loose or bad connection.

**Step 10 — Sensor Isolation:** Disconnect the sensor wire.

- If alarm stops: sensor or wiring is faulty. Reference resistance chart (Figure 4 in M-218-11R — same chart as water temperature sensor).  
- If alarm continues: problem is computer or wiring.

**Step 11 — Computer Connector P2:** Inspect connector P2 for loose pins or corrosion.

**Step 12 — Computer Terminal:** Hydraulic oil temp sensor wiring: **P2-Pin 17**.

**Step 13 — Wiring Short:** Check entire wiring harness from sensor to computer for short to ground.

### Q: Gate sensor failure alarm on display (left or right gate)

**Step 1 — Identify Which Side:** Note which side the digital display indicates — left gate or right gate. On a fixed hopper, only the left gate is used.

**Step 2 — Physical Inspection:** Locate the gate transducer on the indicated side.

- (a) Check if the harness connector is disconnected at the sensor.  
- (b) Inspect for broken or pinched wiring along the transducer harness.  
- (c) Check for bad, corroded, or loose connections at the connector.

**Step 3 — Identify Transducer Type:**

**Solid State (Red/Blue/Black wires):**

- Ensure gate is fully closed.  
- Do not unplug the sensor — check voltage at the connector using Etnyre adapter Part \# 7050350 to avoid stripping wires.  
- Red wire to black: should read **5.0 VDC** (supply, not adjustable).  
- Blue wire to black (gate closed): should read **0.5–1.5 VDC** (ideal 1.0 VDC).  
- If not within spec: loosen transducer mounting bolts and rotate transducer body until blue-to-black voltage is 0.5–1.5 VDC. Retighten bolts (see Figure 5 in M-218-11R).

**Resistor Type Potentiometer (Orange/Green/Black wires — older units):**

- Ensure gate is fully closed.  
- Unplug sensor from harness.  
- Check OHMS across orange and green wires at connector: should read **250–270 OHMS**.  
- OHMS increase as gate opens — do not exceed 270 OHMS.  
- Make adjustment if not within spec.

**Step 4 — Re-calibrate After Adjustment:** After any transducer adjustment, re-run gate null/scale calibration in computer Setup screens. Material will also need to be re-calibrated.

**Step 5 — Wiring Continuity:** Check wiring continuity between transducer and computer connector.

**Step 6 — Computer Connector Terminals (Rexroth):**

- Fixed hopper / Left gate (+): **P1-Pin 10** (Red)  
- Left gate (−): **P1-Pin 18** (Black)  
- Left gate signal/wiper: **P1-Pin 6** (Clear)  
- Right gate (+): P1-Pin 11 (Red)  
- Right gate (−): P1-Pin 19 (Black)  
- Right gate signal/wiper: P1-Pin 7 (Clear)

### Q: One gate opens or closes faster than the other side

**Step 1 — Adjust Gate Speed in Computer Setup:** Enter setup screens (hold CAL \+ turn ignition On; release after 3 beeps). Navigate using the scroll switch to the gate speed screens:

- **Right gate open:** Adjust amps up/down. Higher amps \= faster gate. DO not exceed **1.2 amps**.  
- **Right gate close:** Adjust if needed (1.2 amps max).  
- **Left gate open:** Adjust if needed. On a fixed hopper, this is the primary gate speed control.  
- **Left gate close:** Adjust if needed (1.2 amps max).

Note: On fixed hopper, left gate open/close controls the gate speed. Variable width units: if amps are not the same between left and right, gate fluctuation can occur.

**Step 2 — Hydraulic Pressure Check:** Check and verify hydraulic pressure at the gate relief valve. If pressure is not at 1,500 PSI, adjust per gate relief valve procedure (see Section 8 of this KB).

**Step 3 — Wiring Connections at Gate Valve:** Inspect wiring connections at the gate solenoid valve connector:

- Terminal 1 (Red wire): power to valve  
- Terminal 2 (Black wire): ground

**Step 4 — HON Connector Check:** Locate the Harting (HON) connector on the left outside conveyor rail (and under right conveyor above right front tire for variable units). Check for loose, corroded, or damaged connections.

**Step 5 — Computer Output to Gate Valve:** Measure amp output to the gate valve and compare to the threshold set in computer setup. Output should match. Check computer connector terminals:

- P4-Pin 5: Right gate open solenoid (+)  
- P4-Pin 6: Right gate close solenoid (+)  
- P4-Pin 3: Left gate open solenoid (+)  
- P4-Pin 4: Left gate close solenoid (+)  
- Ground post (−) for all above.

### Q: Very loud noise when the gate master power is turned on or Off (air gates out of adjustment)

**Step 1 — Identify Source:** Determine if this is an air gate problem or a hydraulic gate buss arm problem.

- Loud bang/click at all gates simultaneously \= likely hydraulic buss arm issue → go to Step 3\.  
- Noise at individual gates \= air gate adjustment issue → go to Step 2\.

**Step 2 — Adjust Air Gates:**

a. Back Off ALL hydraulic gate buss arm adjustment screws between all hydraulic gate buss arms and air gates. b. Adjust air gate adjustment screws to achieve a **uniform 1/16" gap** across the entire spreadroll. Ensure air pressure is applied, forcing the gate closed during this adjustment. c. Check all buss arm assembly bolts — they must be tight. Replace any bent or broken bolts. d. Re-calibrate gate null/scale in computer setup (see Section 8). e. With machine running and hydraulic pressure applied to the hydraulic gate buss arm: turn air gate master power On, turn all individual air gate switches On, and allow air gates to open to the hydraulic gate buss arm. f. Adjust hydraulic gate buss arm adjustment screws, forcing air gates to close until the air gate adjustment screws touch and STOP movement. Do not force screws past contact — this will change the transducer setting. g. Re-verify gate null is 0.00 and scale is 4.00.

**Step 3 — Adjust Hydraulic Gate Buss Arm:** a. Re-calibrate gate null/scale in computer setup first. b. If transducer adjustment is also needed, refer to the gate sensor failure procedure above.

### Q: The spreadroll speed is fluctuating / speed is not steady

**Step 1 — Auxiliary Standby Pressure:** Check the auxiliary pump standby (load sense) pressure. If too low, the pump will not come on stroke fast enough, causing insufficient pressure and flow to the spreadroll. Verify standby pressure is set to **400 PSI**. Adjustment procedure is in the operation manual (see Appendix D of M-213-16 and Section 8 of this KB).

**Step 2 — Spreadroll Speed Adjustment:** Check and adjust the speed of the spreadroll (target **100 RPM**) at the flow control cartridge. On standard fixed hopper units, this cartridge is located in the gate/spreadroll manifold (between the conveyors).

- Turn cartridge adjuster CLOCKWISE to decrease spreadroll speed.  
- Turn COUNTERCLOCKWISE to increase speed.

**Step 3 — Flow Control Cartridge Inspection:** Remove and inspect the spreadroll flow control cartridge for contamination or debris. Clean thoroughly and reinstall.

**Step 4 — Pressure vs. Speed Correlation:** Monitor auxiliary standby pressure with a gauge while watching spreadroll speed. If spreadroll speed follows pressure fluctuations (speed drops when pressure drops), this confirms a load sense issue. Proceed to Step 5\.

**Step 5 — Load Sense Check Valves:** Remove the two load sense check valves located on TOP of the conveyor manifold (standard units) and inspect for contamination, debris, worn springs, or pitted seat assemblies. Replace as needed.

### Q: Hydraulic standby / load sense pressure is fluctuating (needle on gauge is not steady)

This occurs when the volume of oil in the load sense system is too great, due to a missing or contaminated orifice or load sense check valve.

**Step 1 — Orifice Inspection (at auxiliary pump):** Locate the load sense line fitting attached to the auxiliary hydraulic pump compensator valve (see Figure 9 in M-218-11R).

- (a) Verify the orifice is installed inside the fitting — it should be a set screw with a **.040 inch hole** drilled through it.  
- (b) Check the orifice for contamination or clogging.  
- (c) Verify the orifice size is .040 inch.

**Step 2 — Check Compensator Operation:** Verify the compensator is responding to hydraulic demand by checking standby pressure with engine running.

**Step 3 — Isolate Load Sense Check Valves:** a. Shut engine Off. Remove the load sense lines from the compensators and cap them Off. b. Start engine and check standby pressure — if pressure is now steady at 400 PSI, proceed to step (b). If pressure is still erratic, the pump or compensator is the issue. c. Reconnect load sense lines ONE AT A TIME to identify which manifold block contains the contaminated check valve. d. Remove and inspect the bad load sense check valve. Clean or replace.

### Q: Gates will not operate (hydraulic buss bar does not move)

**Step 1 — Arming Switches:** Verify that the gate arming switches (gate selector switches on operator panel behind joystick) are turned On. Without the arming switches on, the thumb switch cannot open the gates.

**Step 2 — Fuses/Circuit Breakers:** Check ALL fuses and circuit breakers. Verify there is power on BOTH sides of each fuse holder or circuit breaker — a blown fuse will have power on only one side.

**Step 3 — Gate Override Test:** Press the gate override button and check if the gate operates.

- Gate OPERATES with override: there is an electrical issue → proceed to Steps 5, 6\.  
- Gate does not operate with override: there is a hydraulic issue → proceed to Step 4\.

**Step 4 — Hydraulic Gate Valve:** Manually override the gate valve spool assembly to test hydraulic operation.

- Gate OPERATES with mechanical override: electrical issue → proceed to Step 5\.  
- Gate does not operate with mechanical override: check hydraulic pressures (see Appendix D of M-213-16).

**Step 5 — Gate Transducer / Potentiometer:** Check gate pot/transducer adjustment per Problem 6 and 7 procedures above.

**Step 6 — Gate Null/Scale Calibration:** Verify gate null/scale calibration per operation manual setup procedure.

**Step 7 — Joystick Center Roller Micro Switch:** Check the joystick center roller micro switch — verify it is activating when the joystick is pushed out of neutral.

**Step 8 — Gate Switch in Joystick:** Check power across the gate switch (thumb switch) in the joystick handle — verify it is activating (shows power when pressed).

**Step 9 — Relay Check:** Inspect relays \#2, \#3, and \#4:

- Relay \#2: activates when joystick is pushed out of neutral (micro switch connects), then allows relays \#3 and \#4 to activate with arming switches On and gate switch On.  
- Relay \#3: activates the LH gate.  
- Relay \#4: activates the RH gate. Verify gate arming switches are On during relay testing.

**Note on Gate Hold Feature (variable hopper):** The RH gate uses a gate hold feature set in computer setup screens, designed for straight-line start when spreading rock. If the FPM reading is malfunctioning, the hold feature may prevent the RH gate from opening. Disable the gate hold (set to 0 in setup screens) to allow RH gates to operate in manual mode.

### Q: The starter cranks but the engine will not start

**Step 1 — Display Check:** Does the display show "ENGINE CAN" (no CAN bus communication)?

- YES → proceed to Step 2\.

**Step 2 — Emergency Stop Button:** Check the emergency stop button (Ref 40\) — verify it was not accidentally bumped or pushed in. Twist it clockwise to pop out and release.

**Step 3 — Engine Controller:** Cummins QSB and Tier 3 engines have an Engine Controller that requires power to start. Check if this is present on the machine.

**Step 4 — ECM Fuses:** Locate the Cummins ECM controller fuses near the power and ground distribution hub at the right rear corner of the Cummins engine. There may be up to 6 fuses depending on engine size.

- Verify fuse ratings against the machine schematic.  
- Replace any blown fuse with one of the SAME rating — never use a larger fuse.  
- **Special note for yellow rubber-insulated glass fuse holders:** Pull holder apart and confirm the ring is over the END of the fuse (not just touching the metal part). Ensure a good solder joint at the wiring end. This type of holder can appear intact while actually losing contact under load.

**Step 5 — E-Stop Switch Connections:** Check the E-Stop switch connections for a possible wiring issue related to the fuel solenoid circuit.

**Step 6 — Fuel Solenoid:** Check for an issue with the fuel solenoid on the Cummins engine. If the solenoid is not energizing, fuel cannot reach the injectors.

### Q: Machine stops while I am moving and then moves again when I push the joystick to neutral and back forward

This indicates the neutral joystick micro switch is opening while the joystick is out of neutral — the computer thinks the joystick is in neutral and applies the parking brake.

**Step 1 — Micro Switch Adjustment:** Locate the neutral micro switch on the joystick assembly (see Figure 14 and Figure 15 in M-218-11R). Adjust the neutral micro switch UP so the switch does not open when the joystick is out of neutral. The switch must remain closed within the first 5% of joystick movement in both forward and reverse.

---

### Q: Machine will not go into reverse

**Step 1 — Reverse Micro Switch Check:** The reverse micro switch on the joystick must actuate within the first **5% of joystick movement** in reverse. Locate the reverse micro switch (Figure 14/15 in M-218-11R).

- Adjust the reverse micro switch Down to make it activate sooner.  
- Or adjust the neutral switch to ensure both the neutral and reverse circuits complete within 5% of joystick movement in reverse.  
- The reverse switch (Red/White wire after update) must receive **12 VDC** within 5% of joystick movement in reverse.  
- The neutral switch (Purple wire) must lose **12 VDC** within 5% of joystick movement in forward and reverse.

---

### Q: Machine will not move forward or in reverse after a Rexroth computer update

**Step 1 — Calibrate Joystick:** After any computer update, the joystick must be re-calibrated.

1. With ignition Off, hold CAL switch Down and turn ignition to On. Wait for 3 beeps, release CAL.  
2. Navigate setup screens to **Setup: JOYSTICK**.  
3. Press CAL Down once — screen changes to neutral calibration.  
4. Press CAL Down again — screen changes to forward calibration.  
5. Push joystick FULL FORWARD until display shows 4.0–4.9 volts. Press CAL Down.  
6. Pull joystick FULL REVERSE — display should show \-4.0 to \-4.9 volts. Press CAL Down.  
7. Return joystick to neutral. Screen returns to joystick calibration screen.  
8. Navigate to Save AND Exit screen. Press Save.

**Step 2 — Micro Switch Adjustment:** If machine still does not move after joystick calibration:

1. Turn ignition On. Watch the main display screen after computer boots.  
2. Push joystick forward (out of neutral) — the speed set point display should change to **0**. If it does not change to 0, the neutral micro switch is not activating within 5% — adjust the neutral switch closer to the activating cam.  
3. Push joystick in reverse — speed set point should again change to 0\. If not, adjust both neutral and reverse switches as described in the reverse switch procedure above.

### Q: No gradability — machine cannot climb the hill

**Step 1 — Grade Calculation:** Verify the actual % grade of the incline against the machine's rated gradability at maximum combined weight (80,000 lbs):

- 2WD: **6–8%**  
- 4WD with 107cc motors: **8–12%**  
- 4WD with 160cc motors: **12–18%** Motor size (cc) is stamped on a tag attached to the top of the drive motor. If the grade exceeds the machine's capability, the machine cannot climb it — do not attempt to force it.

**Step 2 — 2WD Machines — Servo Connector Test:** Disconnect the electrical connector attached to the front drive hydraulic motor servo (secure wire away from moving parts). Test the unit on the grade with 80,000 lbs total weight. If performance improves, the servo was receiving incorrect signal — reconnect and further diagnose servo control circuit.

**Step 3 — 4WD Machines — Servo Connector Test:** Disconnect electrical connectors from BOTH front and rear drive hydraulic motor servos. Test on grade at 80,000 lbs. If performance improves, reconnect servos one at a time to identify which motor servo is receiving bad signal (the internal swash plate of the hydraulic motor is being incorrectly positioned by the servo signal).

**Step 4 — POR Pressure Check:** Check and verify the hydrostatic priority override (POR) hydraulic pressure. Target: **6,500 PSI**. Low POR means the system destokes before reaching full torque.

**Step 5 — Forward High Pressure Check:** Check main hydrostatic high pressure (forward). Target: **7,000 PSI**. Low forward relief pressure means reduced drive force.

**Step 6 — Reverse High Pressure Check:** Check main hydrostatic high pressure (reverse). Also target **7,000 PSI**.

### Q: Emergency — Machine is broken down in the road and cannot move

Use the Emergency Driveline Disengage Procedure (M-218-11R). This is for emergency use only — to move the machine extremely slowly Off the road or onto a transport trailer.

**Step 1:** Install two 04MB x 04MJ fittings (Etnyre Part \# 9410200\) — one in each test port of the high-pressure distribution block bolted to the side of the drive pump (remove the 04MB plugs and install new fittings on both sides of the pump).

**Step 2:** Install one 04 x 65-inch hydraulic hose with 04FJX fittings (Etnyre Part \# 3181497\) across between the two fittings. This creates a loop on the drive system, bypassing the pump and relief system.

**Step 3:** Disable or release the park brake unit (mounted in front of the drive motor on the front axle).

**Step 4:** If the engine does not run (or the pump cannot supply brake release pressure): use a porta-power modified with a 04 FJX connection to supply approximately **280–400 PSI** to the parking brake unit to release it.

**Step 5:** If the engine RUNS: place controls in Drive mode, push joystick in direction of movement, and steer as needed. Move the machine VERY SLOWLY (crawl speed only) Off the road or onto the trailer.

**WARNING:** This procedure is for emergency situations ONLY. Not intended for travel or long-distance movement. Damage can occur if not done correctly.

### Q: MDC fault code shows "Invalid Reset"

**Cause:** Some part of the MDC resets without a full CPU reset — typically an internal fault.

**Step 1:** Check if P3-pins 11, 12, 13, and/or 14 are shorted to ground. If 3 or 4 of these pins are grounded simultaneously, an invalid reset occurs.

**Step 2:** Check wiring to the back light of the display for a short or ground fault.

---

### Q: MDC fault code shows "Invalid Speed"

**Cause:** Speed feedback is reading greater than 2,500 FPM — beyond the computer's valid range.

**Fix:** Check for a bad speed pickup (sensor) or electrical noise on the sensor signal line. Check sensor clearance (should be 1/16") and wiring shielding.

---

### Q: MDC fault code shows "Internal Voltage"

**Cause:** One of the regulated voltages (especially the \+5 VDC supply) is out of range.

**Fix:** The \+5V supply powers old-style displays, the joystick, and gate pot power. Check if \+5V is shorted to ground. If a circuit is drawing more than 150 milliamps (old display), this fault will also trigger.

---

### Q: MDC fault code shows "Invalid Program"

**Cause:** Program was not loaded properly into the MDC, or parts of the program have become corrupt.

**Fix:** Reload the program software to the MDC.

---

### Q: Auxiliary hydraulic pressure will not relieve

This is typically discovered when checking auxiliary pump pressures. If the main pressure does not relieve, the auxiliary pump cannot destroke — this causes heat buildup, engine torque loss, and hard starting.

**Step 1:** Shut Off machine.

**Step 2:** Close and shut Off the main hydraulic suction valve.

**Step 3:** Locate and cap Off the case drain at the auxiliary pump.

**Step 4:** Remove the compensator from the hydraulic pump(s).

**Step 5:** Remove the load sense compensator cartridge.

**Step 6:** Locate the 2mm allen head orifice inside the housing (see Figure 12 in M-218-11R). Remove the orifice.

**Step 7:** Clean the orifice using a strand of primary wire or a torch cleaning kit (if strand is small enough to fit). Do not enlarge the orifice.

**Step 8:** Re-install orifice, cartridge, and compensator in reverse order.

**Step 9:** Uncap and reinstall the case drain hose.

**Step 10:** Open the suction valve.

**Step 11:** Install an air regulator to the hydraulic tank through the vent filter connection. Apply **3–5 lbs of pressure** to the hydraulic system to push any trapped air out.

**Step 12:** Start machine and operate all hydraulic functions to ensure full oil circulation. Check and adjust pressures per the operation manual. Verify pressure relieves after main pressure is achieved.

**Note:** If pressure cannot be adjusted on the pump after this procedure, the pump may have sustained internal damage and will need to be replaced. Isolate each pump individually to identify which pump is affected.

## Section 13 — Troubleshooting — Output Quality Problems

### Q: There is an overlap or heavy streak of material down the center of the road when the hopper is fully extended

**Cause:** The material is heavy in the middle — the gate cut-Off plate needs to be adjusted inward.

**Fix:**

1. Locate the gate cut-Off plate in the hopper (see Figure 10 in M-218-11R).  
2. Slide the cut-Off plate **further IN** (toward center) on both sides evenly.  
3. Adjust both sides equal amounts to keep material distribution symmetric.

---

### Q: There is a void or streak down the center of the road

**Cause:** Not enough material reaching the center. The gate cut-Off plate is too far in, or the hopper position is too narrow.

**Fix:**

1. Adjust the gate cut-Off plate by sliding it **further OUT** of the hopper.  
2. If the cut-Off plate cannot be adjusted further out, adjust the hopper cylinder rod end to extend the hopper width (both sides evenly).  
3. See Figure 11 in M-218-11R for cylinder adjustment reference.

---

### Q: The application rate on the ground does not match what the display shows

**Cause:** Material calibration is needed, or the wrong aggregate size is selected.

**Fix:**

1. First verify the correct aggregate size is selected on the display (Section 8).  
2. Perform the full material calibration procedure (Section 8\) with the canvas and scale.  
3. Average at least 3 canvas samples before adjusting calibration.  
4. If calibration cannot bring rates to match within 2 lbs/yd², check for gate wear plate adjustment needs or gate transducer issues.

---

### Q: Gates are delivering material at low speeds but not maintaining proper rate at high speeds

**Cause:** The gate opening is at its maximum 4-inch travel for the aggregate size before reaching the required application rate — the computer cannot open the gate further.

**Fix:**

1. Reduce the speed set point — lower speed requires smaller gate opening, giving the computer more range to control rate.  
2. Check if the maximum gate opening is set correctly in Setup screens (Setup: Gate Opening — should be 4.00" for standard units, 5.00" for big chipper).  
3. Check gate wear plates — if worn, the effective gate opening is reduced.

---

### Q: Material is bridging in the hopper — flow stops intermittently

**Cause:** Aggregate is arching across the hopper and stopping flow.

**Fix:**

1. Ensure the hopper agitator is engaged (agitator disconnect bolt installed).  
2. For sand or small aggregate: install the optional second agitator.  
3. Verify the agitator is rotating — check the agitator drive connection.  
4. Increase conveyor speed to push more material through and break the bridge.  
5. Use the Gate Override button to momentarily open gates fully — this can dislodge bridged material.

---

## Section 14 — Troubleshooting — Application-Specific Problems

### Q: Machine loses traction on a wet or freshly oiled surface

**Fix:**

1. Activate the **Traction Control Switch** (31) — this transfers torque to the front axle. The joystick must be in neutral to engage it. Speed is limited to 200 FPM while engaged. Move the machine into better traction, then disengage.  
2. If available, activate the **Traction Boost Switch** (45) — this pushes up on the hitch to add weight to the rear drive wheels (only effective when connected to a truck). Note: this reduces truck braking efficiency.  
3. Adjust tire pressure within the specified range (55–60 PSI front, 60–65 PSI rear).

---

### Q: Machine cannot hold position on a grade / rolls back when stopping

**Cause:** Parking brake may not have engaged because the speed sensor did not read zero (machine still moving slightly when stopped).

**Fix:**

1. Use the **Brake Pedal** (47) to hold the machine on the grade until the parking brake sets.  
2. Place the **Park/Drive switch** in Park position — this applies the parking brake positively.

>   
> **WARNING:** Parking Brake meets SAE J1472. Parking brake may not hold on grades steeper than 15%. On such grades, chock the wheels.

---

### Q: Spreading on shoulders — material not distributing evenly to the shoulder edge

**Fix:**

1. Use the individual gate selector switches (43, 44\) to turn Off gates that do not cover the shoulder — concentrate material flow to the working side.  
2. Adjust the left and right conveyor speed independently to control where material accumulates in the hopper.  
3. Adjust the conveyor hood position to direct material distribution toward the shoulder side.

---

### Q: Truck is having trouble keeping up / truck is pushing the chipspreader on a downgrade

**Fix:**

1. Under most conditions, the chipspreader should tow the truck.  
2. On steep downgrades where the truck needs to assist in braking, the truck should always set its own brakes after stopping — regardless of grade orientation.  
3. Establish a clear communication system (radio, hand signals, horns) between chipspreader and truck. Each truck driver must know who gives signals, where to look, and what each signal means.  
4. To disconnect from the truck: push Hitch Release button while momentarily pulling joystick rearward to create slack, then drive forward while holding the release button.

---

### Q: Stopping distances are too long / machine cannot stop quickly enough

**Fix:**

1. Slow down — use lower speed set points when towing heavy trucks, on steep grades, or when material is loaded.  
2. Use the brake pedal to supplement hydrostatic braking.  
3. Avoid loading the front hopper or rear hopper when roading the machine — weight increases stopping distance significantly.  
4. The larger the truck and steeper the grade, the longer the stopping distance.

---

### Hopper Spreadroll Wear Plate Adjustment
Turn spreadroll and conveyors “off”.
1. Loosen all spreadroll wear plate hold-down bolts and adjust the wear plate until a nominal 1/16” clearance exists between the wear plate and the spreadroll for the entire hopper width.
2. Re-tighten all the hold-down bolts.

### Hopper Gate Wear Plate Adjustment
Turn spreadroll and conveyors “off”.
1. Loosen wear plate hold down bolts and extend the plate 1/32” past the gate edge along the entire gate width.
2. Tighten hold down bolts.
3. As plate wear occurs, additional adjustment will be necessary.

### Conveyor Belt Centering
1. If the conveyor belt tends to move towards one side of the conveyor, tighten tail pulley adjustment on that side until the belt is running in the center.
2. Should it be impossible to obtain centered belt operation by adjusting the tail pulley it will then be necessary to adjust the head pulley as outlined below. Only a small amount of head pulley adjustment should be necessary to center conveyor belts.

#### For the right hand conveyor:
a) Loosen the four bolts holding the right hand side head pulley bearing.
b) Loosen adjusting bolt jam nuts.
c) Start conveyor at this time.
d) If belt runs to the right hand side of the conveyor, tighten the adjusting screws until the belt is centered on the head pulley.
e) If belt runs to the left hand side of the conveyor, loosen the adjusting screws until the belt is centered on the head pulley.
f) Re-tighten adjusting screw jam nuts.
g) Stop the conveyor belt.
h) Tighten head pulley bearing bolts.

#### For left hand conveyor:
a) Loosen the four bolts holding the right hand side head pulley bearing.
b) Loosen the adjusting bolt jam nuts.
c) Start conveyor at the time.
d) If belt runs to the right side of the conveyor, loosen the adjusting screws until the belt is centered on the head pulley.
e) If belt runs to the left side of the conveyor, tighten the adjusting screws until the belt is centered on the head pulley.
f) Re-tighten adjusting screw jam nuts.
g) Stop the conveyor belt.
h) Tighten head pulley bearing bolts.

> **Warning:** Conveyor must be running during this procedure. To avoid personal injury, be sure to remain clear of moving belt.
> **Warning:** Remain clear of moving parts.

### Conveyor Belt Tension Adjustment
- During first few weeks or when slipping, tighten both sides equally to prevent head pulley slippage.
- Excessive belt tightness will result in shortened belt and pulley bearing life.

## Section 17 — Winterizing / Storage

No specific winterizing procedure is published in the M-213-16 manual. The following is based on general hydraulic/engine machine best practices consistent with the manual's maintenance guidance:

1. **Drain/flush the engine cooling system** if storing in below-freezing conditions. Fill with the appropriate concentration of antifreeze (see engine operator's manual). The anti-freeze decal (Part 3102065-E) on the machine indicates the correct fill specification.  
     
2. **Top Off the hydraulic reservoir** to the sight-eye level to minimize condensation in the tank during storage.  
     
3. **Run the engine to warm** all hydraulic circuits before shutdown, ensuring all moisture is dispersed through the system.  
     
4. **Change engine oil and filter** before long-term storage — contaminated oil during storage accelerates corrosion.  
     
5. **Fully retract all hydraulic cylinders** (gate cylinders, hitch cylinder) to protect cylinder rods from corrosion.  
     
6. **Protect all exposed metal surfaces** with an appropriate corrosion inhibitor.  
     
7. **Disconnect the battery** (or install a battery tender) if storing for more than 30 days to prevent battery discharge. Note: always place the Park/Drive switch in Park before disconnecting power to avoid draining batteries accidentally.  
     
8. **Chock all four wheels** and engage the parking brake before leaving unattended.  
     
9. **Install the locking control box cover** to protect controls from weather and vandalism.  
     
10. **Check tire pressure** before storage and re-check monthly — cold temperatures lower tire pressure.  
      
11. **Upon return to service:** Follow the Hydrostatic System Startup procedure in Appendix D of M-213-16 if the hydraulic system was opened for any maintenance during storage.

### Chipspreader Front Hopper Spreadroll Specifications & Straightening Procedures

The most common cause of the spreadroll becoming bent is careless handling of the hopper.

#### Spreadroll Specifications
- The spreadroll length varies based on the hopperette size and style.
- The spreadroll diameter is 6.00 inches, with a minimum diameter of 5.75 inches.
- Once this minimum limit is exceeded, the air operated gates will not reach the spreadroll properly with the gate wear plates in the correct (flush) placement. Extending the wear plates outward will cause the air gates to not function properly. (See the operator's manual for proper hopper gate wear plate adjustments.)

#### Spreadroll Straightening Procedure
The following procedure can be used in most cases to straighten the spreadroll. On some units, it might be necessary to replace the spreadroll if the straightening procedure fails.

1. Loosen the bearing shaft set screws on both ends of the spreadroll.
2. Determine the high place on the spreadroll at three places: 2 feet in from each end and the middle. This can be done by spinning the spreadroll and marking it with any suitable marker. 1/8" total is the maximum allowable movement for the spreadroll.
3. Using an acetylene-oxygen torch with a small tip (or equivalent heating device), heat the spreadroll on the high side at the 3 places that are marked. Starting with the center, heat to cherry red about 3/4" in diameter, then heat the ends in the same manner as the center.
4. Let the spreadroll cool.
5. Re-check the straightness as in Step 2, and if it is still not straight within 1/8", repeat Steps 3 and 4.
   - Do not heat in the same place; move approximately 2.00 inches to either side.
   - Do not heat the spreadroll in more than a 3/4" diameter spot, or it will warp the roll beyond repair.
   - Take your time when heating so that the heat moves in an even path around your heat spot.
6. When letting the spreadroll cool, let it do so naturally. Do not force the cooling by using water or any other coolant.



### Diesel Exhaust Fluid (DEF), Tier V

Source: Cummins Service Bulletin 4021566, "Diesel Exhaust Fluid (DEF) Specifications for Cummins® Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR) Systems" (released 27-Aug-2012, last modified 22-Jan-2021).

This section provides the specifications, usage, and handling requirements of Diesel Exhaust Fluid (DEF). Other names for DEF are:
- UreaAqueous
- Aqueous Urea Solution (AUS 32)
- AdBlue™
- NOx Reduction Agent
- Catalyst Solution
- Stableguard™ 32

DEF used on Cummins® engines **must** meet the requirements outlined in the specifications below. For further information, reference the DEF manufacturer's Material Safety Data Sheet.

**Note:** Cummins Inc. is not responsible for failures or damage resulting from what Cummins Inc. determines to be abuse or neglect, including but not limited to: operation without correctly specified DEF, lack of maintenance of the aftertreatment system, improper storage or shutdown practices, and unauthorized modifications of the engine and aftertreatment system. Cummins Inc. is also not responsible for failures caused by incorrect DEF or by water, dirt, or other contaminants in the DEF.

#### 1. DEF Characteristics and Specifications

**Warning:** Diesel exhaust fluid (DEF) contains urea. Do not get the substance in your eyes. In case of contact, immediately flush eyes with large amounts of water for a minimum of 15 minutes. Do not swallow. In the event the diesel exhaust fluid is ingested, contact a physician immediately. Reference the Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) for additional information.

**Caution:** Never attempt to create DEF by mixing agricultural grade urea with water. Agricultural grade urea does not meet the necessary specifications, and the aftertreatment system may be damaged.

DEF has the following characteristics:
- Nontoxic and nonpolluting
- Nonflammable
- Stable and colorless
- Odor of slight ammonia
- Composed of urea and water
- Biodegradable

The urea content of DEF solution **must** be 32.5 percent ± 0.7 percent by weight. It **must** meet International Standard ISO 22241-1 for diesel engines. There is no acceptable substitute.

For engines using SCR operating in the United States and Canada, DEF certified by the American Petroleum Institute (API) is recommended. A symbol on the container or dispensing system ("API American Petroleum Institute Certified Diesel Exhaust Fluid") indicates certification.

**Never** add water, or any other unauthorized fluid, to the DEF tank. The aftertreatment system may be damaged and the DEF solution quality will be compromised.

**Caution:** Do not add any chemicals or additives to the diesel exhaust fluid in an effort to prevent freezing. If chemicals or additives are added to the DEF, the aftertreatment system may be damaged.

DEF freezes at approximately -11°C (12°F). The DEF system is designed to accommodate these temperatures and does **not** require any intervention by the owner or operator.

#### 2. Handling, Storage, and Shelf Life of DEF
For detailed information on handling, transportation, and storage of DEF, reference ISO 22241-3.

**Handling:**
- DEF is **not** hazardous, but short-term exposure can cause acute irritation. Proper personal protective equipment should be used when handling DEF. For more information, refer to the First Aid section.
- Select materials may become corroded if contact with DEF occurs for an extended period of time:
  - Carbon steel, zinc coated carbon steel, and mild iron
  - Nonferrous metals and alloys: copper, copper alloys, zinc, and lead
  - Solder containing lead, silver, zinc, or copper
  - Aluminum alloys
  - Magnesium and magnesium alloys
  - Plastics or metals coated with nickel
- If material contact with DEF occurs, clean the surface immediately. See the Disposal and Cleaning and the Contamination and Incorrect Fluid sections.

**Storage:**
- **Only** approved containers should be used to transport and store DEF. Containers made of polyethylene, polypropylene, and stainless steel (Grade 316) are recommended.
- Some DEF containers include a paper seal under the cap. The seal will degrade over time and will contribute to DEF contamination. These containers **must** be identified and inspected regularly once opened to prevent contamination.

**Shelf Life:**
- Many factors affect DEF shelf life. Temperature and duration are major contributors. **Always** check the concentration of DEF, or replace DEF with new, if DEF quality or expiration is questionable.
- DEF has an expected shelf life of 18 months minimum when stored under the following conditions:
  - Storage temperature between -5°C and 25°C (23°F to 77°F)
  - Sealed containers
  - Avoiding direct sunlight
- Shelf life is reduced by 6 months for each 5°C (9°F) increment above the recommended temperatures. For example, 30°C (86°F) = 12 month shelf life, 35°C (95°F) = 6 month shelf life, and so on.
- Long-term storage in a vehicle (in excess of 6 months) is **not** recommended. If long-term storage is necessary, periodic testing of the DEF is recommended to make sure the concentration does not fall out of specification. See the Testing section.

#### 3. DEF Cleanliness Practices
Materials that come into contact with DEF **must** be free from any contamination, oil, fuel, dust, detergents, and any other chemicals.

Containers, funnels, and other equipment that will handle or store DEF should be cleaned before use. Wash thoroughly and rinse with distilled water. If distilled water is unavailable, rinse with tap water, then rinse with DEF.

Follow these recommended practices to avoid contaminating DEF during routine maintenance tasks:
- Clean the DEF tank before opening the DEF tank for filling.
- Clean the dispensing nozzle before filling the DEF tank.
- Keep the dispensing nozzle in the DEF tank during filling to minimize drops and mishandling.
- Ensure proper filtration is used for DEF tank venting to atmosphere.
- Close DEF ports during operation or repair.
- Clean out dust and debris before using a coupler/insert connection.
- Ensure a proper DEF storage container is being used and inspected as necessary. Refer to the Handling, Storage, and Shelf Life section.
- Conduct periodic inspections and eliminate areas where DEF has crystallized.

#### 4. Contamination and Incorrect Fluid
**Never** add water, or any other unauthorized fluid, to the DEF tank. The aftertreatment system may be damaged and the DEF solution quality will be compromised. This may affect DEF in the following ways:
- Change the DEF concentration levels
- Introduce contaminants
- Change DEF chemical properties
- Alter the freeze point of DEF
- Alter characteristics of the DEF solution

If an unauthorized fluid is added to the DEF tank, contact a local Cummins® Authorized Repair location to determine the appropriate repair direction.

If water has been added to the DEF tank, perform the following steps:
1. Drain the DEF tank.
2. Flush with distilled water.
3. Refill the DEF tank with new or quality DEF.
4. Check the DEF concentration.

#### 5. Testing
The correct concentration of DEF is critical to engine and aftertreatment system health and performance.

To test the concentration of DEF, use the Cummins® DEF refractometer, Part Number 4919554. Follow the instructions provided with the service tool. For detailed instructions on testing DEF, reference ISO 22241-2.

**Note:** When using the Cummins® DEF refractometer service tool, the acceptable DEF measurement specification is 32.5 ± 0.7 percent. This specification takes into consideration the refractometer tool tolerances, variability, and calibration when measuring DEF concentration.

#### 6. Disposal and Cleaning of DEF
- If a small amount of DEF is spilled, rinse and clean immediately with water. Spilled DEF, if left to dry or wiped away with a cloth, will leave a white residue. Failure to clean spilled DEF from a surface may result in an incorrectly diagnosed leak of the DEF dosing system.
- If a large amount of DEF is spilled, do one of the following:
  - Transfer it into a suitable container. Label the container correctly.
  - Cover it using an absorbent material.
- Dispose of DEF according to local environmental regulations. Do **not** empty it into the drainage system. Do **not** empty or release it into surface water.

#### 7. First Aid
Follow these recommended practices when working with DEF. For additional information on the proper handling of DEF, refer to the manufacturer's Material Safety Data Sheet.

DEF is **not** hazardous, but short-term exposure can cause acute irritation.
- Avoid breathing vapor or mist.
- Protect eyes. In case of contact with eyes, flush with water for a minimum of 15 minutes.
- Protect skin. In case of contact with skin, wash with soap and water.
- Do **not** ingest. If ingested, contact a physician immediately.

### Script 1 — Caller Reports a Speed Sensor Error Alarm

**Agent:** "Thank you for calling Etnyre support. I understand you're seeing a speed sensor error on your display. Let's work through this together. First, is the joystick currently in the neutral center position?"

**\[Caller confirms joystick is neutral or adjusts it\]**

**Agent:** "Good. The alarm will sound if the joystick is out of neutral and the machine doesn't move — moving the joystick back to neutral often clears it. If it's still showing the error, let's check the fuses and circuit breakers at your control console. There's also an inline 30-amp fuse on the computer power line. Do any of those look blown?"

**\[Caller checks\]**

**Agent:** "Next, we'll look at the speed sensor itself — it's a magnetic pickup mounted on the front axle motor output. Check that it's firmly mounted, properly aligned with the pick-up gear, and has about 1/16-inch clearance between the sensor tip and the gear. Also check for pinched or loose wiring at the sensor connector. If all that looks good, we can check voltages at the sensor connector. If you need hands-on help, please call our service department at 1-800-995-2116."

### Script 2 — Caller Cannot Get Machine to Move After Computer Update

**Agent:** "After a Rexroth computer update, the joystick calibration needs to be re-run. Here's how to do it. First, make sure the joystick is in neutral and the machine is in Park. Turn the ignition Off. Now hold the CAL switch down and turn the ignition On — you'll hear 3 beeps, then release the CAL switch. You're now in computer setup screens."

**Agent:** "Navigate using the scroll switch until you see 'Setup: JOYSTICK.' Press the CAL switch down once. Push the joystick full forward and hold it — the display should show 4.0 to 4.9 volts. Press CAL down again to save forward. Then pull the joystick fully to reverse — the display should show \-4.0 to \-4.9 volts. Press CAL to save reverse. Return the joystick to neutral. Scroll to the Save and Exit screen and press the Save button. Try moving the machine now."

### Script 3 — Caller Reports Gates Will Not Open

**Agent:** "Let's check the most common causes. Are the gate arming switches turned on? Those are on the operator panel behind the joystick. Both the master gate switch and the individual gate switches need to be on for normal operation. Also, have you checked all the fuses and circuit breakers — including both sides of each fuse holder?"

**\[Caller confirms switches and fuses OK\]**

**Agent:** "Next, press the gate override button and see if the gate operates. If the override works but the thumb switch doesn't, we're looking at an electrical path issue — the relay circuit or joystick micro switch. If even the override doesn't move the gate, we have a hydraulic issue and we'll need to check your gate cylinder hydraulic pressure. For the relay check: relay 2 activates when the joystick leaves neutral, and that allows relays 3 and 4 to open the left and right gates respectively. If you need a technician, call 1-800-995-2116."

### Script 4 — Caller Reports Machine Will Not Climb Grade

**Agent:** "Before we troubleshoot, let's confirm you're within the machine's rated gradability. What's the approximate grade percentage? And is this a 2WD or 4WD machine — and if 4WD, what's the motor displacement?"

**\[Caller provides info\]**

**Agent:** "The rated gradability at 80,000 pounds combined machine, truck, and aggregate weight is: 6 to 8 percent for 2WD, 8 to 12 percent for 4WD with 107cc motors, and 12 to 18 percent for 4WD with 160cc motors. The motor size in cc is stamped on a tag on top of the drive motor. If you're within those limits but still can't climb, the next step is to disconnect the electrical connector on the drive motor servo — this rules out a servo signal issue. If the machine pulls better with the servo disconnected, the servo is receiving a bad command signal. We can also check your POR setting — it should be at 6,500 PSI, and forward/reverse high pressure should both be at 7,000 PSI."

### Script 5 — Caller Reports Loud Noise When Gate Master Switch Is Turned On or Off

**Agent:** "That noise is typically from air gates that are out of adjustment — the air gate is snapping hard against the buss arm when the system pressurizes or de-pressurizes. First, confirm whether the noise happens at all gates at once or at individual gates."

**\[Caller confirms\]**

**Agent:** "Here's the adjustment sequence. Back Off all hydraulic gate buss arm adjustment screws first. Then adjust the air gate adjustment screws to achieve a uniform 1/16-inch gap between the gates and the spreadroll. Make sure air pressure is applied while you do this so the gates are being held closed. Tighten all buss arm bolts — check for any bent or broken ones. After that, re-calibrate the gate null and scale in the computer setup. Null should be 0.00 inches, scale should be 4.00 inches. Then re-run the buss arm adjustment with the hydraulic buss arm energized to get the gate adjustment screws touching the stop. Call us at 1-800-995-2116 if you need a walk-through."

### Script 6 — Caller Reports Water Temperature Alarm

**Agent:** "First, let's find out if the engine is genuinely overheating or if it's a sensor alarm. Do you have a laser temperature gun or mechanical gauge you can use to check the actual coolant temperature at the radiator or engine?"

**\[Caller checks\]**

**Agent:** "If the actual temperature is below 240°F, the alarm is likely a sensor or wiring issue — not a real overheat. Disconnect the sensor and see if the alarm clears. If it does, the sensor resistance is out of spec and needs to be replaced. If the temperature really is above 240°F, shut the machine down immediately and let it cool. Most common causes of genuine overheating on a chipspreader are: low coolant level, debris packed in front of the radiator from chip operations, or the hydraulic cooler and radiator plugged with aggregate dust. Check those first before restarting."

### Script 7 — Caller Reports Spreadroll Speed Is Fluctuating

**Agent:** "Spreadroll speed fluctuation is most often caused by low auxiliary hydraulic standby pressure or a contaminated flow control cartridge. The standby pressure should be at 400 PSI — if it's too low, the pump won't come on stroke fast enough to supply steady flow. Is your standby pressure gauge reading steady at 400 PSI when the spreadroll is running?"

**\[Caller checks\]**

**Agent:** "If pressure is low, the standby adjustment is on the back of the auxiliary pump — use a 5mm allen wrench to turn the set screw clockwise to increase it. If pressure is correct but fluctuating, check the load sense check valves on top of the conveyor manifold — they're the small cartridges on the manifold block. Remove and inspect for debris or a worn seat. Also remove and clean the spreadroll flow control cartridge in the gate/spreadroll manifold. Call 1-800-995-2116 for further assistance."
